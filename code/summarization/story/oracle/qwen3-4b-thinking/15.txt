The story follows Sergeant Major Andrew McCloud, a non-commissioned officer working in Washington D.C., who is thrust into leadership during a mysterious epidemic affecting the United States. The "one hundred per cent American plague" spreads across the country but does not affect international populations, causing widespread fear without panic since no fatalities have occurred. Despite being dismissed by higher-ranking military officials who consider him "irregular," Andy and his corporal, Bettijean Baker, take charge of investigating the outbreak after they're given priority status.

As they analyze reports, they discover the epidemic disproportionately affects creative professionals—writers, artists, poets, college students, new parents, and engaged people—while sparing government workers, doctors, dentists, and postal workers. The pattern leads them to a seemingly absurd but crucial insight: victims are primarily those who lick stamps. Through meticulous investigation, Andy realizes the cause is contaminated four-cent stamps that have been licked by people in small offices and creative fields.
