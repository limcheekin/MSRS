The bagpipes hold profound significance in Isobar Jones' story as both his emotional anchor and a life-saving tool. As a man feeling isolated after six months on Luna, the bagpipes represent his only connection to Earth and his longing for adventure—a simple pleasure that becomes vital when he's most vulnerable. Commander Eagan's order banning musical instruments initially strips Isobar of this solace, making him feel truly trapped in his "goldfish in a glass bowl" existence.

The significance deepens dramatically when Isobar ventures outside to play the bagpipes during a Grannies attack. He realizes the pipes' true purpose: they can be used as an unconventional communication system.
