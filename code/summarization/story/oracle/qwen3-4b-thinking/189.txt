The townspeople's shared dream experience is significant because it serves as a collective coping mechanism and hidden social space that connects them in their otherwise ordinary lives. Throughout the story, Sol Becker discovers that everyone in the small town dreams about Armagon—a mysterious place with a palace, knights, and an execution—every night. This shared dream world becomes a secret community where they can process unspoken issues and maintain a sense of belonging.

The significance lies in how this dream experience creates a parallel reality that functions as both an escape and a social glue for the town's residents.
