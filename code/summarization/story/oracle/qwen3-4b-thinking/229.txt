The setting of Philip K. Dick's "The Hanging Stranger" is a small American town called Pikeville, where Ed Loyce discovers a body hanging from a lamppost in the town square. The story depicts a seemingly ordinary small town with specific local characteristics: a downtown area featuring businesses like Loyce's TV sales store, a hardware store, and a bus line; residential neighborhoods with lawns and apartment buildings; and municipal buildings including City Hall (described as "an old-fashioned yellow structure of wood and gilded brass") and a police station.
