Janis's character is pivotal to resolving the mysterious epidemic in this story. While the team initially struggled to identify the source of the outbreak affecting writers, artists, poets, and small office workers but not government employees or large office staff, Janis provides the crucial clue that unlocks the mystery.

During her hospital visit, Janis reveals she had written and mailed a letter to her mother using a regular stamp. When Andy examines her story in detail, he realizes she must have licked the stamp—something professional men with postage meters or government offices with franking privileges don't do. This simple action explains why small office workers (who handle stamps manually) get sick while government employees and larger offices (with automated systems) remain unaffected.

Without Janis's specific account of licking a stamp, the team would have remained stuck trying to find patterns in the epidemic's spread. Her personal story directly leads to the discovery that the "villain" is actually a four-cent stamp, revealing the epidemic stems from people licking stamps
