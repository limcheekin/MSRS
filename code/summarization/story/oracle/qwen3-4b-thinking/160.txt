The story "Master of Life and Death" follows Roy Walton, an assistant administrator in the Bureau of Population Equalization (Popeek) in a future Earth with seven billion people struggling under overpopulation. As part of Popeek's controversial program, Walton has the power to decide which individuals qualify for "equalization"—a euphemism for euthanasia or termination of life deemed substandard. The plot centers on Walton's moral dilemma when poet Lyle Prior visits him with his two-week-old son
