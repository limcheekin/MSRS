In this story, the terms "tumbler" and "hoofer" describe the physical challenges of returning to Earth after space travel. A tumbler is someone who moves with grace and ease in microgravity—someone who can perform acrobatic movements without difficulty, as Hogey explains when he says, "I used to be a tumbler—huk!" before his return to Earth. This ability stems from his experience in space where gravity is negligible.

In contrast, a hoofer is someone who walks normally on Earth's surface with full gravity—someone who must adapt to Earth's stronger gravitational pull after months in space. When Hogey struggles with Earth's gravity, he says, "now I gotta be a hoofer," highlighting the fundamental shift in his physical capabilities. He describes himself as "a born tumbler" who now has to become a hoofer, meaning he must adjust to walking and moving in Earth's gravity that was previously absent.

The story emphasizes this distinction through Hogey's experiences: he falls repeatedly, stumbles, and has difficulty standing upright because his body has adapted to weightlessness.
