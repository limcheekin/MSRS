Herbert's wife is not given a specific name in the story, but she is clearly portrayed as Mrs. Herbert Hyrel. She holds profound significance in the narrative of escape and desperation as the central figure who embodies the very reality Herbert seeks to flee. 

Throughout the story, Herbert expresses deep hatred toward his wife for her control over his finances and her perceived emotional detachment. He views her as the embodiment of his "trap"—the financial and emotional constraints that make him desperate for escape.
