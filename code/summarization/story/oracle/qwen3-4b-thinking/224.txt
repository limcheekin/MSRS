The story is set against the harsh, desolate landscape of the Great Plains in rural America. This barren, treeless country stretches across rolling hills and wheat fields, creating a stark backdrop for Hogey Parker's difficult return to Earth after nine months in space. The environment is particularly unforgiving—described as "treeless, barren, and rolling," with a dusty, yellow-hued sky that "faded into sulphurous yellow toward the zenith" and carried "yellow smoke" from the pervasive dust of the plains.

As Hogey struggles to adapt to Earth's gravity after his long space career, he navigates this challenging terrain while physically deteriorating. The setting reinforces his disorientation: the sun is described as a "hateful eye-sizzling horror" that "painted everything with pure white pain," and the land feels alien and hostile to someone who has spent so much time in space. The environment isn't just physical but also psychological—Hogey's perception of the world is distorted by his space-induced symptoms, making the familiar landscape feel foreign and threatening.
