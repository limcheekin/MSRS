Michael and Mr. Carpenter share a mentor-mentee relationship rooted in cultural adaptation and mutual guidance. Michael, having recently left the secluded Brotherhood, is unaccustomed to the complex, rule-bound society of Portyork. Mr. Carpenter, a salesman and local guide, serves as his liaison to the customs and tabus of the United Universe. He acts as both an educator and a protector, warning Michael about societal norms—such as hand-baring, public eating, or using the word "marriage"—that would otherwise lead to social or legal repercussions. Though Carpenter initially presents himself as a helpful, respectful figure, his interactions reveal underlying tension: he is protective of the system’s rules, often emphasizing universal tabus with rigid authority. Michael, in turn, struggles with the absurdity and emotional weight of these rules, ultimately rejecting the society's norms when he learns that marriage is forbidden. This leads to his decision to return to the Brotherhood. Their relationship thus reflects a clash between primitive simplicity and universalized civilization—Michael resists the artificial conformity of the new world, while Carpenter upholds it as essential to peace and harmony. The dynamic underscores the story’s central theme: cultural immersion is not just about learning rules, but about confronting the emotional and psychological cost of living in a society governed by shared, often contradictory, tabus.
