The setting of the story is the fictional town of Pikeville, a small, seemingly ordinary community where a subtle and sinister invasion has taken place. The narrative unfolds primarily in the town's central square, surrounding areas like the park, the City Hall, the police station, and local businesses, all of which are gradually overtaken by an alien, insect-like presence. These entities, described as winged beings with human-like mimicry, infiltrate the town’s social and political systems, taking control of minds and institutions, including the police and city government. The story alternates between the surface-level normalcy of daily life—traffic, shops, people going about their routines—and the underlying horror of a hidden invasion. The setting is both literal and symbolic: Pikeville represents a microcosm of society vulnerable to external control, while the town’s physical landmarks—like the lamppost with the hanging body and the dark patch above City Hall—serve as visual metaphors for the breach between reality and the alien presence. The transformation of the town into a controlled environment, complete with mind-controlled citizens and a hidden entrance point, underscores the story's central theme: a quiet, insidious takeover that operates beneath the surface of everyday life.
