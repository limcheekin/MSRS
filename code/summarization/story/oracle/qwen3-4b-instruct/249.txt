The government regulations in the story include strict travel limitations—men are only allowed to go no further than their own property or a neighbor’s, such as Walt and Gloria Shanks’ place—and rationing of essential goods like meat, fuel, and household supplies. These regulations are enforced to maintain control over resources during a post-apocalyptic crisis. Food is delivered via government supply bins, with strict limits on consumption, such as one sinkful of dishwater per day and one bath per week. Education is also tightly regulated, with emergency rules stating children must be five years nine months old before entering kindergarten. Additionally, travel is heavily restricted, and any deviation—like Harry’s journey to the ocean—is considered a violation. These regulations reflect a society in survival mode, where the government controls daily life to preserve a small, isolated community. The story implies that these rules are not just practical but are part of a larger system of control, as seen when the doctor reveals that the world has been reduced to a surviving "ark" of three people who have engineered a new society, wiping out memory and emotion in others to maintain stability. The regulations thus serve both as survival tools and as mechanisms of psychological and societal control.
