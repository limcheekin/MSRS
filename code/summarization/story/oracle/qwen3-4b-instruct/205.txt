The plot of the story centers on Martin Isherwood, a young man driven by an unshakable ambition to become the first human to pilot a manned rocket to the Moon. Despite facing widespread skepticism and ridicule from family, friends, and society—such as when his father mocks him as a "rocket pilot" or when others dismiss the idea of man-landing on the Moon—Marty remains steadfast in his dream. He dedicates his life to mastering aerospace engineering, navigation, and mathematics, working through low-level jobs, flying planes, and training rigorously. His obsession with the rocket mission becomes all-consuming, leading him to isolate himself from relationships and personal interests. The story reveals that his ambition is not just about technical achievement but a deep, almost spiritual yearning to reach the Moon and fulfill a personal destiny. Ultimately, he succeeds in landing on the Moon, but the journey is emotionally and psychologically taxing. The narrative concludes with a tragic twist: due to concerns about his mental stability, a psychiatrist administers hypnosis to erase the emotional intensity of the mission, making it feel like a routine task. After the mission, Marty is deeply disillusioned, and he dies on the Moon, symbolizing the cost of pursuing extraordinary ambition at the expense of human connection and emotional balance. The story explores the tension between ambition and sanity, showing how a man’s pursuit of something extraordinary can consume him entirely.
