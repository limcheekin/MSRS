The setting of the story is a secluded, isolated island off the coast of Maine, surrounded by rocky cliffs, narrow coves, and dense forests. The island is remote and untouched by modern life, with a quiet, almost timeless stillness that contrasts sharply with the outside world. A small, white Cape Cod cottage sits on the landward side of a rocky spine, surrounded by thick shrubbery and a neatly maintained lawn. The only signs of human activity are a vintage Essex car parked in the driveway and a radio that emits old-fashioned, static-filled broadcasts—seeming to play pre-recorded news from the 1930s. The island is cut off from the mainland, with the girl, Mary Alice Pope, living there alone with her aunts, who maintain a strict isolation from the outside world. The environment is both beautiful and eerie—peaceful yet tinged with an uncanny sense of timelessness, as if the island exists outside the normal flow of time. The setting enhances the story’s central mystery: the girl's apparent existence in a world frozen at 1933, despite the present day being 1953, suggesting a deeper supernatural or psychological connection to the past.
