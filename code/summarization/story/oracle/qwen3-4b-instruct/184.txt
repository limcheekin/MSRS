The old man in the story symbolizes the quiet, enduring presence of the past and the cost of survival in a harsh, unforgiving environment. As a Kumaji elder who lived among the Earth colonists, he represents the forgotten history of coexistence and mutual dependence between the two groups. His decision to stay in the deserted village, despite the danger and lack of water, underscores his acceptance of mortality and a deep sense of belonging to the place—his only true home. His silence and resignation contrast with Steve’s active confrontation of the past, highlighting the difference between personal vengeance and peaceful resolution. The old man's final words—"Tell the people... hurry... Kumaji looking for them to kill"—serve as a grim reminder of the violence that has driven displacement and loss. His presence forces Steve to confront not just the tragedy of the poisoned well, but also the moral weight of his own actions and choices. Ultimately, the old man embodies the tragic fate of those who remain behind, unable to escape history, and whose quiet suffering becomes a powerful lens through which the story examines guilt, memory, and the enduring human need for home.
