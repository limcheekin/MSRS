The setting of the story is a distant, alien world called Godolph, located within the third ring of the Galaxy near its center. This planet is characterized by its unique environmental and social conditions: weather is artificially controlled, with constant rain that makes human navigation hazardous, and transportation relies on a flowing "transport tide"—a ribbon of water that carries natives swiftly and silently through the city. The world is governed by strict regulations, particularly concerning interstellar travel, where no ship bound for the central Galaxy will accept a passenger without an identification tab. This creates a system of uncertainty and isolation for travelers, especially those stranded due to schedule delays. The city itself is described as eerie and dangerous for humans, with narrow walkways, dark alleys, and a society that views outsiders as eccentric or insane. The environment amplifies the sense of danger and helplessness, making even simple movements risky. The story unfolds in this alien, bureaucratic, and physically hostile world, emphasizing the psychological and physical challenges faced by a lone human navigating an unfamiliar, hostile galactic ecosystem.
