Shano’s occupation as a laborer in the Plutonian mines and his physical condition—marked by arthritic joints, gum-clogged lungs, and chronic coughing—reveal his resilience, practicality, and deep-rooted connection to manual labor. These traits shape his actions throughout the dangerous journey home. His experience in hazardous mining environments, where he survived toxic gas exposure due to his clogged lungs, gives him a unique understanding of the dangers of toxia gas and the value of physical endurance. This knowledge directly informs his decision to enter the engine room and manually repair the damaged selector valves, using his body as a tool to sustain the ship’s operation. Despite being an old man dismissed by others as insignificant, Shano acts with quiet determination and resourcefulness, demonstrating that his laborer’s instincts—practical, hands-on, and grounded in survival—enable him to overcome the crisis. His actions show not just physical strength, but a deep-seated sense of agency and purpose, proving that even in the face of overwhelming danger and isolation, he is capable of decisive, heroic intervention. His journey home is not merely physical; it is a testament to the enduring value of human labor and resilience in the face of technological and existential threats.
