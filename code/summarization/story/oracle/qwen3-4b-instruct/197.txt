The seal-people in the narrative serve as a crucial metaphor for adaptation and survival in an alien environment, embodying both biological and cultural evolution. Their physical traits—such as their aquatic lifestyle, unique dwellings, and sensory adaptations—highlight how life forms evolve to thrive in extreme conditions. More significantly, the story reveals that the symbiote invading Kaiser’s body mirrors the seal-people’s own biological symbiosis, suggesting a deeper connection between the alien environment and its inhabitants. The seal-people's progression from seemingly primitive, odoriferous beings to more intelligent, human-like entities demonstrates a form of evolutionary adaptation driven by environmental necessity. This parallel underscores the narrative’s central theme: survival and adaptation require not just physical changes, but also shifts in cognition and behavior. Kaiser’s increasing resemblance to the seal-people—his enhanced perception, altered emotions, and growing empathy—illustrates that adaptation is not solely biological but also psychological. The significance of the seal-people thus lies in their role as a mirror reflecting the transformative power of symbiosis and environmental pressure, showing that survival in alien settings demands not only resilience but also transformation of self.
