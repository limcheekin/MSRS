Fred Walton is Roy Walton's younger brother and a doctor employed by the Bureau of Population Equalization (Popeek). His significance in the narrative lies in his role as a moral and familial counterpoint to Roy. While Roy grapples with a personal ethical dilemma—spared one baby from euthanasia—Fred represents the institutional and bureaucratic framework that both enables and constrains such decisions. Fred’s discovery of Roy’s actions, including his unauthorized alteration of a child’s medical record, reveals the extent to which the moral compromise has been exposed within the system. His sharp, suspicious nature and direct confrontation with Roy underscore the tension between personal conscience and institutional loyalty. Moreover, Fred’s position in the Happysleep clinic places him directly within the machinery of population control, making his knowledge of Roy’s betrayal a critical threat to the Bureau’s integrity. Ultimately, Fred symbolizes the internal corruption and familial conflict that underlie the moral crisis at the heart of the story—highlighting how even within a system designed for rational population control, personal ethics and familial bonds can undermine its cold logic.
