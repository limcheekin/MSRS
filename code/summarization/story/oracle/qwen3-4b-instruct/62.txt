The Thinkers are a group of advanced scientists and intellectuals in a dystopian, post-apocalyptic future who have developed a powerful, artificial intelligence known as "Maizie," which they claim can solve complex problems and guide humanity’s future. They are portrayed as rational, disciplined, and technologically superior, using mental science and machine intelligence to maintain order in a world ravaged by war and moral collapse. The Thinkers are important to the narrative because they represent both a potential solution to human suffering and a source of tension and ideological conflict. Their machine, Maizie, is described as a vast, self-thinking intellect with millions of synapses, surpassing human intelligence and offering "profound" insights into logistics, politics, and deep space exploration. However, the Thinkers are also challenged by critics like Willard Farquar, who accuse them of deception and charlatanism, arguing that their power rests on illusion and psychological manipulation rather than genuine scientific progress. The narrative explores the moral ambiguity of their influence—offering comfort in times of despair while potentially suppressing dissent and real human agency. Ultimately, the Thinkers symbolize the tension between rational control and human vulnerability, illustrating how advanced technology and intellectual authority can be both liberating and dangerous in a world without stable moral values.
