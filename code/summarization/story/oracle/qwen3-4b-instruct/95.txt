The story follows Michael Frey, a young man who leaves a secluded Brotherhood community to enter the diverse and complex society of the United Universe. After years of studying universal customs and tabus, Michael is unprepared for the bewildering array of cultural norms he encounters in Portyork, the largest spaceport. He faces numerous social missteps—such as wearing bare hands, speaking about eating in public, using the word "history," or mentioning marriage—each of which violates specific tabus enforced by the Universal Kinship System. These rules, derived from a global consensus to prevent conflict, apply universally across all planets and species, making even minor infractions potentially serious. Michael's growing discomfort with these rigid social codes leads him to question the very foundation of the society he once admired. Ultimately, he decides to return to the Brotherhood, realizing that the structured, isolated life within his community offers a more authentic and emotionally safe existence than the superficially civilized but emotionally alienating world of universal conformity. The story critiques the idea of universal harmony through rigid rules, highlighting how such systems can suppress individuality and genuine human connection.
