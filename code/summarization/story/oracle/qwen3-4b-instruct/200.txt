The plot centers on Professor Venetti, a government scientist working on a method to neutralize radioactive waste, who is unexpectedly approached by gangster Tony Carmen, who requests his invention to dispose of bodies. Initially reluctant due to ethical and legal concerns, Venetti agrees to help, creating a device that destroys matter without producing energy—violating the law of conservation of energy. The machine, which he calls the "Expendable," works by reducing mass to zero, effectively annihilating the material. Carmen plans to commercialize the invention, renaming it the "Expendable" and marketing it as a revolutionary garbage disposal device. He leaks information to the public, sparking widespread interest. However, the U.S. Atomic Energy Commission (AEC) discovers that the machine increases Earth's temperature by converting destroyed matter into heat, posing a catastrophic environmental threat. The AEC bans the device, but Venetti argues that mass psychology would lead people to continue using it despite the danger. In response, he invents a "Disexpendable" device that consumes excess energy, reversing the original machine’s effect. Though the Disexpendable is inefficient, it offers a solution. Ultimately, the story reveals that the invention, though scientifically flawed and ethically problematic, becomes a symbol of both human ingenuity and its potential for misuse, highlighting the tension between innovation, control, and environmental responsibility.
