The strange aspect of this harsh and rapidly aging world is that life is so fleeting—lasting only eight days—that every moment is lived with extreme urgency and intensity. From birth, individuals are thrust into a cycle of rapid aging, where childhood, adolescence, and even maturity happen in flash-like moments. People die within hours or days, and their bodies visibly deteriorate, like wax melting under fire. This unnatural speed of life and death creates a world where emotions, relationships, and social interactions are forged in seconds, with no time for growth, reflection, or genuine connection. The inhabitants live in constant fear of dying, which shapes their actions—driving them to fight, steal, or love intensely. Even basic human experiences like laughter, friendship, or conflict arise from immediate, instinctual reactions rather than deep understanding. The world is not only physically harsh but psychologically devastating: people are trapped in a cycle of terror and fleeting existence, where memory, identity, and hope are compressed into moments that feel like dreams. This makes the entire experience of life profoundly strange—beautiful yet tragic, short-lived yet deeply felt.
