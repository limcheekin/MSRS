Technology in the society of *Time in the Round* serves as both a tool of control and a mirror of cultural values, shaping children’s understanding of history, violence, and identity. The advanced technology—particularly the Time Theater and its "Time Bubble"—is designed to provide safe, passive access to historical scenes, preventing children from experiencing real conflict or trauma. Uninjes, artificial dogs programmed with canine-like behaviors, symbolize a sanitized version of nature and emotion, reflecting the society’s effort to eliminate real violence through conditioning. Children are prevented from engaging in physical combat or even being hurt, as their bodies and actions are "contraprogrammed," reinforcing a world where harm is impossible. This technological suppression of pain and conflict underscores a broader societal belief that violence is unnatural and must be eradicated through education and automation. However, the Butcher’s defiant desire to reintroduce warfare—his belief in a world where people actually fight—reveals a deep resistance to this technological pacification. His fascination with Huckleberry Finn and his imagined dirtiness suggest a longing for authenticity and raw human experience, which technology has deliberately removed. Thus, technology in this society functions not just as a convenience, but as a mechanism of emotional and psychological conditioning, shaping children’s perception of history and their place within it—keeping them safe, but also detached from the complexities and realities of human conflict.
