The significance of Garve leaving the ship and following the mysterious allure of the enchanting yet perilous city lies in his embodiment of the city's dangerous influence and its connection to the ancient Legend of Eric the Bronze. Unlike Eric, who is initially cautious and uses the metal hat as a mental shield, Garve acts on instinct and curiosity, entering the city without protection. This decision highlights the city’s power to captivate and corrupt, transforming him into a living symbol of its allure. His descent into the city reveals that the city does not merely affect individuals through illusions—it actively shapes their identity, leading Garve to believe he is part of a prophesied destiny. The city’s influence turns him into a distorted version of himself, reflecting the fear and delusion that permeates the narrative. Ultimately, Garve's journey underscores the central theme: the city is not just a physical place but a psychological force that tests the boundaries of reality and identity. His fate—being drawn into the city and later becoming a target of its inhabitants—demonstrates that the allure is seductive and perilous, and that those who succumb to it are consumed by its illusions. Garve’s actions thus serve as a cautionary tale, illustrating how even a seemingly rational person can be undone by the power of belief and desire.
