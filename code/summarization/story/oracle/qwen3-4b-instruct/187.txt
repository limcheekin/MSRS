Mrs. Brundage is the wife of Vincent Brundage, a man who died of a heart attack in the town of Armagon, a place where dreams and reality intertwine. In the story, she is portrayed as a grieving widow who regularly dreams about the palace in Armagon, where an execution takes place. Her husband, Vincent, is believed to have died under tragic circumstances, though the town's residents, including the Sheriff and Mr. Dawes, keep the details of his death private. Mrs. Brundage is central to the town’s dream world—she is one of many who visit Armagon nightly, and her emotional state reflects the town’s collective dream-life. When Sol Becker questions her about her husband’s death, she reveals that he died of a heart attack, and she insists on keeping the matter private. The events surrounding her involve the town's residents, who all share dreams of Armagon, where a mysterious "execution" occurs. Mrs. Brundage's role underscores the story’s central theme: reality and dream are indistinguishable in this peculiar town, where people live in a shared, dreamlike world that blurs the line between life and fantasy. Her grief and her nightly visits to Armagon symbolize the town's collective unconscious, making her a key figure in the town’s enigmatic, dream-based existence.
