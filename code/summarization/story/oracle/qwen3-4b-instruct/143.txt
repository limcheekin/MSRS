Individuals who lack special abilities, or psi-deficients, are viewed with pity, misunderstanding, and social exclusion in this world where most people possess unique powers. They are seen as anomalies, "throwbacks" to an earlier era, and are often labeled as defective or useless. Family members and society at large assume they are inherently inferior, with the belief that their lack of psi abilities makes them unfit for meaningful roles. The story illustrates how such individuals are marginalized—denied jobs, excluded from social and professional life, and treated as burdens. Even when capable of valuable actions, like healing, their abilities are unrecognized or dismissed due to the societal emphasis on psi powers. Despite this, the narrative reveals that these individuals possess hidden strengths—most notably, the power of healing—which are not tied to telepathy or telekinesis but to empathy and physical care. The story ultimately redefines how such people are valued: not by their lack of powers, but by their capacity to heal, comfort, and contribute meaningfully to human well-being. Thus, the world begins to recognize that being without psi abilities does not equate to worthlessness, and true value lies in human connection and care.
