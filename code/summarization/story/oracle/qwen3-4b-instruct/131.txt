John Smith is interested in the various kinds of holes he perceives because they are central to his theory about ancient history and time dimensions. He believes that holes serve as physical gateways or portals through which one can study the past and different time dimensions firsthand. His fascination stems from the idea that these holes—whether in walls, bottles, or the Earth itself—are not merely gaps but dimensional passages that allow access to other times and realities. Specifically, John had fallen through a hole in a force-field-lacking area while returning to the university to prove his theory that musical instruments existed before recorded tapes. He lost his connection to his own time dimension, and now he must find the correct hole to return to his original timeline. His obsession with holes reflects both his scientific curiosity and his deep personal stakes: if he fails to return, he will be discredited and his entire research effort will be seen as invalid. Thus, the holes are not just objects of observation but vital pathways tied to his identity, credibility, and survival across time.
