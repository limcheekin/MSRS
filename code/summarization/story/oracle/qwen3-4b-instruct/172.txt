The melting sickness is a fatal, contagious disease that once decimated the human colony on Minos, killing nearly all inhabitants except members of the Mead family. It was not caused by a known microorganism, as no pathogens were found in the bodies of the victims. Instead, the disease appears to have been a result of a biochemical incompatibility between human cells and the planet’s environment. The illness is linked to the planet's unique chemistry—specifically, the use of left-handed amino molecules in its food, which are indigestible by humans. The only way to survive on Minos was through a radical genetic adaptation process initiated by Alexander P. Mead, who evolved human leucocytes (white blood cells) over thousands of generations to digest the alien food. Once adapted, these cells were reintroduced into the Mead family, allowing them to thrive. However, the disease remained latent and infectious, as evidenced by the death of test hamsters injected with blood from a carrier. The melting sickness is thus not a traditional infection but a systemic biological incompatibility that required genetic evolution to overcome. This makes it a unique and terrifying threat—silent, invisible, and only survivable through a specific, irreversible genetic transformation.
