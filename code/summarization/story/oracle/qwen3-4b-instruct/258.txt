Captain Midas and Mister Spinelli share a complex, antagonistic relationship rooted in greed and mutual suspicion. Both are driven by the desire to profit from the mysterious derelict ship, which is revealed to be constructed of a transmuting metal that converts living beings into gold. While Captain Midas initially claims sole ownership of the treasure, he secretly fears Spinelli's ambition and attempts to control him by withholding information about the gold. Spinelli, however, openly challenges this control, asserting that he discovered the derelict and therefore deserves its full reward. His declaration—"I found her, and she's mine!"—exposes his direct confrontation with Midas’s authority. As the story unfolds, their relationship deteriorates into one of betrayal and violence; Spinelli betrays Midas by revealing the gold to Zaleski and Cohn, seeking personal gain. When the derelict begins emitting erratic signals and the crew shows signs of decay, Spinelli becomes increasingly suspicious, ultimately leading to a violent confrontation in which Midas kills him. Their dynamic reflects the story’s central theme: greed corrupts even the most rational men, and the pursuit of treasure leads not to wealth, but to moral decay and tragedy. Ultimately, Spinelli’s actions confirm that he is as consumed by greed as Midas, though his rebellion reveals a darker, more dangerous form of ambition.
