In a society where conformity is paramount and individuals are identified by numeric designations, the State acts as a totalitarian regulator that enforces uniformity, suppresses individuality, and controls personal identity through rigid bureaucratic systems. Citizens are stripped of personal names and assigned numerical designations that reflect their status and function within the state apparatus. This system ensures total compliance by making any deviation—such as a desire to change one’s name or pursue personal relationships—a potential threat to social stability. The State punishes nonconformity through mechanisms like psycho-scanning, assignment to remote penal colonies (e.g., Marscol), or social ostracism. Even basic human needs, like mating or joining social groups, are restricted because such activities would imply autonomy and individual choice. The State maintains control not only through surveillance and enforcement but also by embedding conformity into daily life—through regulations, euphemistic language, and the internalization of state values. In this context, the State is both the guardian of order and the suppressor of human freedom, rendering personal identity obsolete in favor of functional, predictable roles. The narrative illustrates how such a system creates profound psychological isolation and despair, as individuals like the protagonist are emotionally and socially paralyzed by fear of retribution. Thus, the State's role is not merely administrative but deeply oppressive—ensuring that individuality is erased and obedience is absolute.
