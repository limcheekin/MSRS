Doctor Hamming is a reclusive, elderly psychiatrist who runs a hidden, post-apocalyptic sanctuary known as "the world" — a self-sustaining, isolated community built from survivors of a global holocaust. In the context of a man's struggle with his sanity and the realities of a changed world, Doctor Hamming embodies both a solution and a terrifying paradox. He presents himself as a healer, offering treatments like diathermy to "cure" mental instability and restore sanity to individuals like Harry Burr. However, his true nature is revealed as manipulative and authoritarian: he has constructed this world by selecting only the most resilient survivors—primarily farmers—and systematically erasing memories of chaos, death, and reality from their minds. He claims to have saved humanity by isolating it from the devastation, but in doing so, he has created a false reality where people believe they live in normal times, while in truth, the world is a meticulously controlled simulation. His traits—cold rationality, obsession with control, and denial of the true scale of destruction—highlight how a man’s perceived sanity can be manufactured through institutionalized deception. The story thus explores how trauma, memory, and societal collapse can fracture a person’s sense of reality, and how such fractures may be suppressed not by healing, but by creating an artificial world where madness is eradicated at the cost of truth.
