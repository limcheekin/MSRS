Larry Dobbin is Willard's best friend and fellow space traveler aboard the ship *Mary Lou*. He dies in the story, having spent four years in space and succumbing to illness and the profound loneliness of isolation. As he nears death, Dobbin experiences a hallucination of the "Ghost Ship," a legendary vessel said to appear to space travelers who have died alone in space. He claims to have seen it, saying, "It's come—for me!" This moment marks a turning point, as Willard begins to question the nature of solitude and the existence of the Ghost Ship. Ultimately, Dobbin dies, and Willard is left alone in space, haunted by memories of Earth and the lingering presence of the Ghost Ship. Though Willard initially dismisses the legend, he later realizes that the Ghost Ship may be a manifestation of the emotional and existential loneliness of those who live forever in space. In the end, Dobbin's death and the haunting vision of the Ghost Ship become symbolic of the human condition—of being isolated, forgotten, and ultimately, part of a spectral existence that lingers in the vastness of space.
