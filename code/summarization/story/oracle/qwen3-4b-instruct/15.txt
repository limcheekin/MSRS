The plot revolves around a mysterious epidemic that spreads across the United States, affecting civilians but not government workers, military personnel, or those in large offices. The outbreak initially baffles officials, who suspect a foreign germ attack or sabotage. Sergeant Major Andrew McCloud, a noncommissioned officer, is thrust into leadership after being relieved of his duties by higher-ranking officers, but he quickly takes charge and works with Corporal Bettijean to investigate the cause. Through meticulous data collection and analysis, they identify that the illness disproportionately affects individuals in small offices, writers, artists, poets, and new parents—groups known for frequently handling and licking stamps. The key clue is a four-cent stamp found in a sick girl’s purse, which leads to the discovery that the illness is linked to licking stamps, likely due to a chemical contaminant in the adhesive. The story culminates in McCloud and Bettijean revealing the cause to the military leadership, recommending public warnings against stamp licking, investigating stamp production safety, and disseminating medical treatment. The solution is both clever and grounded in everyday human behavior, highlighting the importance of observation, teamwork, and thinking outside conventional boundaries in crisis situations.
