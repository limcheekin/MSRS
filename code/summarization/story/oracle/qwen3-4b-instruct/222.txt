The other humans, or "hoofers," help Hogey get home by offering practical and compassionate support throughout his journey. When Hogey arrives at the bus stop, the driver helps him by guiding him safely across the road and later assists him by taking him in a car to Marie’s father’s place. A farmer, recognizing Hogey’s situation, helps him by driving him directly to the Hauptman farmhouse, despite initial suspicion that he is a tramp. The farmer also shows kindness by ensuring Hogey has a safe and timely arrival. When Hogey is nearly stuck in a concrete pile after tripping, the dog Hooky, belonging to Marie’s brother, senses his distress and approaches with affection, offering emotional comfort. Though not physically rescuing Hogey from the concrete, the presence of the family—through the farmer and the dog—signals a warm, accepting community that understands and supports him despite his disorientation and physical struggles. These human interactions, rooted in empathy and familiarity, provide the emotional and logistical help Hogey needs to reach home, reinforcing the story’s theme that even a man broken by space can find belonging among Earth-bound "hoofers."
