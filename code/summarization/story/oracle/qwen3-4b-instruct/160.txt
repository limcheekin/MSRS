The plot of *Master of Life and Death* centers on Roy Walton, an assistant administrator of the Bureau of Population Equalization (Popeek), a futuristic bureaucracy tasked with controlling overpopulation through strict measures such as sterilizing subnormal individuals and euthanizing children deemed genetically unfit. As Walton carries out these cold, impersonal decisions, he faces profound moral dilemmas when he encounters Lyle Prior, a poet whose son has been recommended for euthanasia due to a suspected tubercular gene. Despite knowing the program is legally and ethically mandated, Walton is moved by the emotional weight of the situation and secretly alters the child's medical file to spare his life. This single act of defiance—saving one child—shatters the bureaucratic system he serves, exposing the moral cost of enforcing a dehumanizing policy. As he acts, he becomes aware that both the director, FitzMaugham, and his brother, Fred, know of his betrayal. The story explores the tension between societal survival and individual humanity, ultimately questioning whether any system designed to save civilization can truly uphold human dignity. Walton’s decision marks a personal rebellion against a mechanistic future, revealing that even in a world governed by logic and efficiency, compassion remains a vital, dangerous force.
