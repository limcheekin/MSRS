The significance of the army in the story lies in its role as a symbol of necessary, albeit overdue, action in the face of an existential threat. The peaceful colony, deeply conditioned to avoid conflict and despise soldiers, initially resists the arrival of the army, viewing it as an intrusion on their idyllic way of life. However, the army represents the only force capable of responding to the alien threat—something the colonists, due to their pacifist upbringing, are unprepared or unwilling to do themselves. Captain Dylan, a veteran soldier, embodies the contrast between peace and preparedness: he has spent years waiting, drifting, and being dismissed, yet he is the one who recognizes the urgency of the situation. His arrival forces the colonists to confront reality, shifting their perspective from passive innocence to active survival. The army’s presence thus serves as a catalyst for change, breaking the illusion of safety and compelling the people to take decisive, albeit painful, action. Ultimately, the story highlights that peace, while noble, can be dangerously fragile without the strength and readiness to face inevitable threats—making the army not just a military force, but a moral and practical necessity.
