The story revolves around Herbert Hyrel, a man living in a bleak, desperate future where people are emotionally and financially drained, turning to illegal telportation technology to escape their realities. Hyrel is consumed by resentment toward his wife, whom he blames for his financial hardship and emotional neglect. His hatred grows into a violent obsession—specifically, a desire to kill her so he can seize her wealth. This rage is symbolized by his fixation on a "bottle of rare old wine," representing the delayed, almost ritualistic pleasure of fulfilling his revenge. However, the plot takes a shocking turn when Hyrel is not only denied the escape he craves but is instead murdered by his wife, who uses the same telporter technology to take control of the situation. In a chilling reversal, she becomes the true agent of liberation, ending his suffering by having him "unstoppered" from his own delusions and killing him in cold, calculated fashion. The story explores the futility of revenge and the illusion of escape, showing how both the physical and emotional realities of people are manipulated by technology and power. Ultimately, it reveals that true freedom comes not from fleeing reality, but from confronting one’s deepest flaws—especially when those flaws are rooted in hatred and dependency.
