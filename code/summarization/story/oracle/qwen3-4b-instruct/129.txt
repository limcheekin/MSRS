Isobar Jones is a lone meteorological officer stationed at a remote lunar outpost, Luna III, where he serves as the station’s primary weather observer. Initially, he is portrayed as a somewhat irritable and homesick man, deeply discontent with the monotonous, isolated life of the lunar station. His only source of comfort is his bagpipes, which he plays in secret to ease his loneliness. However, when Commander Eagan issues a strict order banning all musical instruments due to disturbances in the Dome’s operations, Isobar becomes furious and defiant. Despite being ordered not to play, he secretly plans to go outside to play his pipes, a move that defies both protocol and safety rules.

His actions take a dramatic turn when he ventures outside the Dome, bypassing security, to escape the confines of the station. After wandering into a lunar valley, he encounters Patrolmen Roberts and Brown, who have been ambushed by the deadly, indestructible Grannies—lunar creatures with armor-hard exoskeletons. Realizing their dire situation, Isobar improvises: he plays his bagpipes to create a sound that carries through the Dome’s ventilation system. He believes the sound will alert the station’s staff, prompting them to search for him and ultimately send help.

To his astonishment, the Grannies stop their attack and freeze in place, listening intently to the music. They appear to be deeply affected, showing signs of calm and even pleasure. This proves that the Grannies can hear—and are responsive to—sound. Isobar’s pipe-playing becomes a vital lifeline, drawing the attention of Sparks, who sees them from his turret. The armored tank arrives just in time, rescuing the trio. In the end, Isobar’s actions—driven by desperation and a deep emotional need for connection—save the lives of the Patrolmen and demonstrate that even in the most alien and hostile environments, music and human emotion can have profound, unexpected power.
