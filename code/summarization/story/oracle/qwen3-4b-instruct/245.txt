The story follows Harry Burr, a man grappling with unsettling memories and a distorted reality in a world shaped by mysterious regulations and personal loss. After weeks of disorientation, fragmented memories, and growing fear—such as forgetting his son Davie or the existence of livestock—he begins to question his perception of reality. The world around him feels wrong: his home is misaligned, his farm layout is unfamiliar, and he encounters impossible details like an ocean in a rural area or people who don’t exist. These distortions peak when Harry rides his horse north, only to be stopped by police for breaking travel regulations. He is taken to a sterile, gray medical facility run by Dr. Stan Hamming, who reveals the truth: the world has been devastated by nuclear holocaust, and Earth is now largely uninhabitable. The survivors—only a few hundred people—have been isolated in a self-sustaining, artificial "ark" constructed by Dr. Hamming and his sons. They live in a controlled, sanitized environment, with memories of normal life artificially implanted to ensure sanity. Harry’s confusion and trauma stem from the fact that he is not just remembering the past, but being fed a false narrative. The story's central plot is Harry’s descent into madness and eventual realization that he lives in a post-apocalyptic refuge where reality has been meticulously rewritten—leaving him to confront the horrifying truth that he, like everyone else, is part of a fabricated world built on denial, control, and survival.
