The relationship between the Elliotts and Mr. Snader is one of wary curiosity and manipulation. Snader presents himself as a mysterious time traveler offering the Elliotts a free, risk-free trial of time travel to help them escape their current struggles—Jeff’s financial and emotional setbacks after losing his television engineering business and Ann’s anxiety over their future. Though initially skeptical, the couple is drawn to the idea of reversing past mistakes, especially Jeff’s missed opportunity five years earlier. Snader positions himself as a guide and opportunist, offering not just a journey through time but a chance to reshape their lives. However, his role is deceptive: he does not merely entertain them; he exploits their desire for change by luring them into a fabricated future where they are immediately absorbed into a corporate system. The Elliotts are ultimately taken in, not by genuine time travel, but by a carefully constructed illusion that serves Snader’s and the Continental Radioptic Combine’s interests—enslaving Jeff as a skilled technician in a time-warping, alternate reality. Thus, their relationship is transactional and predatory, with Snader using emotional vulnerability and the promise of redemption to gain control.
