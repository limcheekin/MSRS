Enslavement in the story serves as a central theme that exposes the hypocrisy and cruelty of power structures, particularly within the religious and political systems of the Misty Ones on Sekk. The slaves, especially female captives like Tholon Sarna, are subjected to horrific rituals—such as being chosen for sacrifice to the god Uzdon—highlighting how enslavement is not only a physical condition but a tool of control, dehumanization, and systemic oppression. This system contrasts sharply with the freedom Noork ultimately achieves through his journey and rebellion. His encounter with the Misty Ones reveals that they too are enslaved by their own beliefs and rituals, reinforcing the idea that enslavement is a cycle perpetuated by fear and superstition. The story uses this to critique how societies justify domination through religion and tradition. Moreover, Noork’s transformation from an amnesiac wanderer to a free agent who rescues Sarna and defeats the oppressive priests underscores that true liberation comes not just from escaping physical bondage, but from breaking the ideological chains of enslavement. Thus, enslavement in the tale functions as both a narrative device and a moral framework, illustrating the struggle for autonomy and the power of human agency to overcome oppression.
