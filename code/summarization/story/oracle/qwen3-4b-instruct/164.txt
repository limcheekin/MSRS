Philip Prior is a poet and a father whose son, Philip, is scheduled for euthanasia under the population control program known as Popeek. Prior visits Roy Walton, the assistant administrator of the Bureau of Population Equalization, seeking to have his son spared from Happysleep due to a potential tuberculosis diagnosis. Though Walton initially refuses, he is deeply moved by Prior’s plea and the poetic implication that if he had been given the chance to live, he might have produced poetry—echoing Keats’ own struggle with tuberculosis. In a moment of moral defiance, Walton secretly alters the medical records to remove the euthanasia recommendation and the tubercular-prone gene marker, effectively saving his son. However, this act makes him a criminal under the Equalization Law and reveals his betrayal of the system. He later discovers that both Director FitzMaugham and his brother Fred are aware of his actions, deepening the moral and personal consequences. Ultimately, Philip Prior is spared, but Walton's rebellion against the program marks a profound internal conflict between bureaucratic duty and human compassion.
