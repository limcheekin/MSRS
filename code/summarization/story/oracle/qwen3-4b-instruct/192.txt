The theme of marriage in *Prime Difference* is explored through the lens of disillusionment, deception, and the fragility of perceived intimacy. The protagonist, George Faircloth, initially views marriage as a source of suffering due to his wife Marge’s constant complaints and emotional manipulation. His desire to escape this dynamic leads him to create a duplicate of himself—an Ego Prime—to take over domestic responsibilities and allow him to pursue relationships with other women. This act reflects a deep distrust in the institution of marriage, particularly when it fails to meet one's emotional or personal needs. However, the story ultimately reveals that Marge was not just passive or jealous—she was observant and intelligent, having long suspected the existence of the Prime. Her realization that the duplicate is not truly "him" exposes a deeper truth: genuine love requires authenticity, not performance. The story critiques the idea that marriage can be solved by technological substitution, showing instead that emotional connection depends on real presence, mutual understanding, and self-awareness. In the end, Marge’s affection for George is not replaced by a machine but deepened through her own insight and growth, suggesting that true marital fulfillment lies in acceptance, patience, and the courage to see one's partner as fully human—rather than as a problem to be fixed.
