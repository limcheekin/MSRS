Sim gains his knowledge and absorbs his surroundings through innate telepathic awareness and direct sensory immersion in a world defined by extreme brevity and danger. From the moment of birth, Sim perceives his environment with an intensity far beyond typical infancy—his mind immediately grasps the harsh realities of life: the rapid aging of his parents, the inevitability of death within eight days, and the cyclical nature of survival in a planet where days are short and life is fleeting. He absorbs knowledge not through language or formal education, but through vivid mental images, emotional resonance, and the observation of patterns in human behavior—such as love, anger, competition, and the formation of enemies or friendships. These insights emerge from direct contact with his parents’ thoughts and the daily rhythms of cave life, including the fleeting moments of freedom during dawn and dusk. Sim’s mind acts like a receiver, picking up and interpreting the emotional and environmental signals around him—like the sight of green plant life sparking a surge of understanding about growth and vitality. Through this continuous mental engagement, he develops a deep, intuitive grasp of existence, forming a worldview shaped by urgency, loss, and the desperate need to survive and understand before time runs out.
