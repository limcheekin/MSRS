The setting of the story is a post-apocalyptic rural landscape that has been drastically altered by global catastrophe and strict government regulations. Once a familiar agricultural community in what appears to be Iowa, the world has been transformed into a fragmented, isolated environment where normal life no longer exists. The land is fallow, with farms abandoned and fields left empty due to emergency rationing, while new regulations govern nearly every aspect of daily life—food, travel, livestock, and even personal memory. The rural farmstead where the protagonist, Harry, lives is now surrounded by unnatural barriers, such as a massive steel-wired fence curving inward, hinting at a world that has been physically and mentally reshaped. The environment itself feels alien: the air is described as sweet and sharp, the ground shifts from sand to wood, and the horizon reveals an ocean that should not exist in this region. This surreal, disorienting landscape mirrors the protagonist’s deteriorating mental state, reflecting a world where reality has been rewritten by survivalist elites who have built a small, controlled society on the remnants of civilization. The setting is both physical and psychological—a decaying countryside and a confined, sterile world of government-run order, where memory, identity, and truth are manipulated to maintain control.
