The significance of time in the story lies in its portrayal as a dimension rather than a linear, unchangeable stream. The characters’ desire to alter past decisions—specifically Jeff’s regret over missing a chance to buy a building five years earlier—highlights their yearning for control and redemption. The protagonist, Mr. Snader, presents time travel not as a way to revise history, but as a journey through a multidimensional framework where events are fixed and time is spatial. The story emphasizes that going back in time does not mean revisiting the same events; instead, travelers encounter different versions of reality where historical events have been restructured or erased. This reframing challenges the belief that past mistakes can be undone, suggesting that time is not a film reel to be rewound, but a complex, multidimensional space where "what happened" is shaped by context and location. Ultimately, the story uses time travel as a metaphor for human hope and desperation—Jeff’s desire to go back reflects his emotional struggle with failure—but the narrative ultimately reveals that such a journey doesn't offer true change. Instead, it forces acceptance of the present, showing that even in the face of impossible or fantastical possibilities, people must live with the consequences of their choices.
