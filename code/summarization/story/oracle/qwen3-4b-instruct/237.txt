Roddie and Ida are adversaries in function but ultimately share a deep, mutual understanding that transforms their relationship from hostile to profoundly human. Roddie, a human raised among mechanical soldiers who experience no pain, hunger, or emotion, initially sees Ida—an Invader who is physically and emotionally vulnerable—as a threat to be eliminated. However, as they journey together through the ruins, Ida reveals her purpose: to help the wounded, to escape the city’s oppressive structure, and to assert the humanity of her people. She challenges Roddie’s rational, mechanical worldview, asserting that emotion, love, and loyalty are not irrational but essential to survival and meaning. Their shared suffering—climbing the bridge, bleeding, starving—creates a bond where both recognize their differences and limitations. Ultimately, Roddie chooses not to kill Ida, realizing that she embodies the same emotional depth and moral purpose he once believed only machines possessed. In doing so, he acknowledges his own humanity and concludes that the city belongs not to superior machines, but to people like Ida—those who endure, feel, and fight with love. Thus, their relationship evolves from conflict to a mutual recognition of shared identity as human beings.
