Barry Barr undergoes a profound physical and psychological transformation throughout his journey on the hostile planet Venus. Initially, he is a grounded Earthman, trained in engineering and driven by personal longing for connection, particularly with Dorothy Voorhees. After surviving a catastrophic accident that exposes him to Sigma radiation, Barry begins experiencing radical bodily changes: his skin develops membranous webs between fingers and toes, and dark lines form on his neck, indicating the emergence of gills. These changes signify a biological adaptation to Venus’s humid, swampy environment, forcing him to transition from air-breathing to water-breathing. As his body evolves, he loses control over his environment—his room is locked, power is cut off, and he must survive by swimming through the alien swamps. This physical transformation is mirrored by a deep psychological shift: he becomes increasingly detached from human norms, confronting isolation, fear, and survival instincts. His initial pride and denial give way to vulnerability, and his romantic hopes—especially his bond with Dorothy—are tested by deception and trauma. Ultimately, Barry transcends humanity’s boundaries, becoming a semi-aquatic being fully adapted to Venusian life, symbolizing both the dangers of alien environments and the resilience of the human spirit in the face of transformation.
