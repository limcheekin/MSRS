The character often referred to as the "chicken colonel" is Colonel Patterson, though the text actually introduces the "chicken colonel" as a later figure—specifically, a young officer who appears with a rigid, authoritative demeanor and a sense of military superiority. This character is initially dismissive of Sergeant Major Andrew McCloud, mocking his rank as a "noncom" and attempting to assert control by relieving McCloud and Bettijean of their duties. However, when the general intervenes and reasserts authority, he overrules the colonel’s disciplinary actions, declaring that the sergeant and corporal will now take command during the emergency. The colonel is then effectively sidelined, and his insistence on hierarchy is rejected in favor of practical leadership. By the end of the story, the colonel’s role is diminished, and he is left in a subordinate position, ultimately powerless in the face of the real crisis and the superior judgment of the sergeant. His presence serves as a contrast to the pragmatic, resourceful leadership of McCloud, emphasizing that competence and adaptability triumph over rigid rank.
