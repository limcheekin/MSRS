The plot revolves around Ed Loyce, a practical man in Pikeville, who discovers a human body hanging from a lamppost in the town square. Despite his growing fear and distress, the townspeople show no concern or reaction—ignoring the body and continuing with their daily lives. This indifference unnerves Loyce, who begins to suspect something is deeply wrong. His investigation leads him to realize that the entire town has been taken over by alien, insect-like beings capable of mimicking humans and controlling their minds. These entities originate from another dimension, infiltrate society through mimicry and mind control, and operate with a deliberate strategy—such as hanging a man from the lamppost as bait to lure an escapee. Loyce, who escaped detection because he was underground at the time, becomes a fugitive and learns that the invaders are not omnipotent, leaving a crack in their control. The story's central mystery is why the body is left there: it is revealed to be bait, designed to draw out someone like Loyce who has escaped, thus confirming the aliens' intent to identify and eliminate those who resist their influence. The unsettling discovery of the body and the people’s complete lack of reaction highlight the theme of societal deception and the fragility of human perception under alien control.
