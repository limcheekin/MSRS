The plot revolves around the Perpetual Order of Seekers After Truth (POSAT), a mysterious organization that claims to hold ancient, transformative knowledge derived from a super-genius founder who lived four centuries ago during the Renaissance. The story follows three individuals—Bill Evans, Miss Elizabeth Arnable, and Donald Alford—who independently encounter POSAT's advertisement promising personal transformation and universal knowledge. Each responds differently: Evans, desperate and unemployed, sees it as a lifeline; Arnable, spiritually inclined, enthusiastically joins; and Alford, a skeptical physicist, is initially doubtful. When they receive follow-up materials, their experiences diverge: Evans finds no help, Arnable is accepted and receives a membership pin, while Alford receives a psychological questionnaire that deepens his suspicion. The climax reveals the truth: POSAT is not a modern secret society but an ancient organization founded by a visionary scientist who discovered and guarded revolutionary scientific principles—such as calculus, relativity, and quantum theory—before their modern rediscovery. Using advanced technology, including a futuristic reactor and a massive computing machine, POSAT aims to understand and manipulate human motivation to prevent societal self-destruction. The story ultimately reveals that POSAT’s true mission is to change human nature through scientific insight, ensuring civilization survives by altering how people think and act—though it does so through secrecy, psychological testing, and selective recruitment.
