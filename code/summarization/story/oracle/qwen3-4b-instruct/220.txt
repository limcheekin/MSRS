The story follows Big Hogey Parker, a former space tumbler returning to Earth after nine months away, as he struggles with physical and emotional disorientation upon re-entry. His body is unaccustomed to Earth's gravity, causing him to fall, stagger, and feel physically ill. Upon arrival, he faces disbelief and confusion from local residents who mistake him for a tramp or hobo. The turning point comes when he learns that his wife, Marie, has remarried and now lives with her brother, John Hauptman. This revelation shatters his sense of identity and belonging. He is overwhelmed by the reality of his absence—his son, whom he believed to be a child born from their future, is now a real, living child. The emotional climax occurs when he discovers his feet are trapped in concrete while trying to reach the house, symbolizing his entrapment in both physical and emotional hardship. The story explores themes of alienation, identity, and the painful transition from a life of space adventure to family life on Earth, culminating in a mix of despair, longing, and resignation. Despite the challenges, the narrative ultimately suggests that the return home is not a triumph but a deeply human, often tragic, experience of reconnection.
