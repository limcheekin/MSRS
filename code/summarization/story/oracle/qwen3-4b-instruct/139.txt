The lockets in the story serve as symbolic tools of personal agency and moral resistance, representing the couple’s right to choose between enduring suffering or ending their lives painlessly. Given to every member of the expedition, they are designed to allow instant, painless suicide if faced with unbearable terror or pain. In the context of the desolate Earth and the society’s collective fear of violent death, the lockets become a powerful challenge to the regime’s control. When Michael and Mary threaten to use them to die before the council, they expose the truth that their society has suppressed the reality of violent death—fearfully avoiding it for centuries. This act forces the people to confront their own trauma and compels them to reconsider their survivalist policies. Ultimately, the lockets catalyze a shift in the narrative: instead of being silenced, the couple’s choice to live and die naturally on Earth becomes a revolutionary act that restores hope and authenticity to life. Their decision to go beyond the city walls and find a living, breathing Earth proves that true renewal begins not with technological salvation, but with human choice and the courage to embrace life—even in desolation. Thus, the lockets symbolize both personal freedom and the moral awakening of a society that has lost touch with reality.
