The story follows Isobar Jones, a man stationed at a remote lunar outpost on Luna, who struggles with profound homesickness and emotional despair after six months of isolation. The outpost, a sterile and monotonous environment, strips him of natural pleasures like grass, flowers, and sunlight, leading to a deep sense of "world sickness" or existential weariness. His only solace is his bagpipes, which he plays in secret to alleviate his loneliness. However, when Commander Eagan issues a strict order banning all musical instruments due to noise disturbances, Isobar becomes enraged and defiant. Rather than accept defeat, he secretly ventures outside the Dome, breaking protocol to experience the fresh air and greenery he longs for. His journey leads him into danger when Patrolmen Roberts and Brown are ambushed by the deadly, indestructible Grannies—lunar creatures immune to weapons. Isobar's only hope is to play his bagpipes to signal the Dome. To his surprise, the Grannies stop their attack and become mesmerized by the music, proving they can hear and respond to sound. The pipes' music not only calms the creatures but also alerts the Dome’s radioman, Sparks, who sees them through the turret window. When he hears the music, he realizes the Grannies are incapacitated, not just stunned. This unexpected turn saves the trio, and Isobar's act of defiance—driven by homesickness and a desperate need for connection—becomes a pivotal moment of both personal and survival significance. The plot ultimately explores how human emotion, creativity, and resilience can overcome isolation and even defeat seemingly invincible threats.
