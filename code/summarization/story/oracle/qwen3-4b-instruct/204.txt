The theme of capitalism is explored through the transformation of the scientist’s revolutionary invention—a waste-disposal device—into a commercial product. Initially developed for government use to solve the problem of radioactive waste, the invention is repurposed by the gangster Tony Carmen as a tool for eliminating unwanted bodies, which he then markets under the name "Expendables." This reflects capitalism’s drive to commodify even unconventional or dangerous technologies. Carmen, a business-minded criminal, recognizes the potential of the device to become a mass-market product, envisioning its use in homes and industries as a revolutionary appliance. He strategically leaks information to the public, creating demand and positioning the invention as a consumer necessity. His plan to sell the device to Arcivox Corporation—highlighted as a company seeking innovative appliances—demonstrates how capitalism values profit over ethics or safety. Even though the invention violates scientific laws and poses environmental risks, the capitalist system embraces it for its market potential. The story ultimately critiques capitalism by showing how such a device, designed for disposal, becomes a tool of both convenience and exploitation, illustrating how profit motives can override moral responsibility, public safety, and scientific integrity.
