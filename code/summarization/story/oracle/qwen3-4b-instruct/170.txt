The plot revolves around a group of explorers who land on the seemingly idyllic planet Minos, only to discover it is already inhabited by a colony of humans who have been genetically adapted to survive there. The colonists, descendants of the Mead family, have evolved through a process of forced cellular evolution—where human cells were subjected to thousands of generations of hardship and alien food to adapt to Minos’s unique biochemistry, including left-handed molecules that make Earth food indigestible. This adaptation explains why the inhabitants, like Patrick Mead, possess a strikingly uniform appearance and are biologically distinct from Earth humans. The explorers initially assume the planet is safe, but their medical tests reveal that the colonists carry a deadly "melting sickness" that killed most early settlers, with only the Mead family surviving due to their genetic immunity. The story raises critical questions about contagion, survival, and biological compatibility—highlighting the dangers of unregulated adaptation and the risks of introducing Earth-based life forms to alien ecosystems. Ultimately, the explorers must decide whether to settle on Minos, knowing that any contact with the colonists could spread a potentially lethal disease, while also recognizing the profound ethical and biological implications of their own potential inability to survive there. The narrative blends science fiction with deep philosophical inquiry into evolution, identity, and the limits of human survival beyond Earth.
