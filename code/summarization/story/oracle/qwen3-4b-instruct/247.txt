Edna and Harry are a married couple living in a post-apocalyptic world where societal collapse has led to extreme rationing, confusion, and a breakdown of memory and reality. Their relationship is strained by Harry’s growing mental instability, which stems from the drastic changes caused by global catastrophe—specifically, the aftermath of nuclear explosions that devastated most of the world. Harry suffers from fragmented memories, including delusions about having a son named Davie and a son who died in the bombing, while Edna is both concerned and confused by his deteriorating mental state. She tries to help him by urging him to see Dr. Hamming, but Harry resists, believing he is not ill and fearing the doctor's influence. Their bond is tested as Harry’s confusion blurs reality—his memories, the layout of their home, and even the existence of towns and people are distorted. Ultimately, Edna represents stability and emotional grounding, while Harry embodies the disorientation of a world where normal life has been erased. The story reveals that their relationship is not just personal but symbolic: Edna stands for the remnants of pre-collapse humanity and memory, while Harry is a victim of a world remade by trauma and survival, reflecting how drastic change fractures both identity and connection.
