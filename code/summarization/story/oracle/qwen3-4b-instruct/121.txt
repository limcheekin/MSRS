The story takes place primarily in two distinct settings: a bustling, modern 1957 restaurant and a mysterious, futuristic home known as Snader’s "station." The restaurant setting reflects Jeff and Ann’s current reality—filled with ordinary suburban life, economic anxiety, and personal struggle over lost opportunities. It serves as a grounded backdrop to Jeff’s emotional turmoil and his desire for escape. In contrast, the second setting is a surreal, time-travel-themed facility located in a quiet neighborhood, which appears to be a hidden hub of temporal technology. This location features a dimly lit waiting room with two moving corridor screens labeled "Ante" and "Post," symbolizing the flow of time as a fourth dimension. The environment is both technological and eerie, with numbered stations, a strange architecture, and an atmosphere of controlled mystery. As the story unfolds, the setting shifts again when Jeff and Ann are transported into a future version of a city—Costa, West Goodland—where everything is unfamiliar, including language, currency, and social norms. This future setting emphasizes the story’s central theme: time travel as a disorienting, complex phenomenon that challenges perceptions of reality, identity, and place. The contrast between these settings underscores the narrative's exploration of personal crisis and the allure of escaping the past through impossible means.
