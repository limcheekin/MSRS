Michael and Mary were sent on a mission to explore the galaxy in search of a habitable planet to replace Earth, which had become nearly uninhabitable due to centuries of environmental destruction and human neglect. After traveling two thousand years through space, they discovered that no other planets in the galaxy were viable for human life—only Earth remained, though it was already degraded beyond recovery. The mission’s true purpose was not just survival but uncovering the truth about humanity’s fate. They found that every expedition to find a new home had failed, with most crews dying in the process due to hostile atmospheres, violent deaths, and the sheer difficulty of interstellar travel. Despite this, the people on Earth had been conditioned to avoid the reality of violent death through propaganda and psychological control, preventing them from facing the truth. Michael and Mary’s revelation—that Earth was the only home and that humanity had failed itself—was meant to shatter the illusion of salvation. Ultimately, they chose to return to Earth not as survivors of a mission, but as witnesses to its decay and a symbol of hope. Their decision to live out their lives on the barren land, despite knowing it would end in death, became an act of defiance and love. They found a single patch of green grass, a sign of life returning, and chose to build a new life together—proving that even in the face of extinction, humanity could rediscover its connection to Earth and each other. Their mission was not to find a new world, but to awaken a lost sense of purpose and belonging.
