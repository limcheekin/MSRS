Steffens decides to engage with the mysterious mechanical beings on the desolate planet because he senses a profound significance in their existence and believes they represent a deeper truth about advanced life beyond human understanding. Initially drawn by the ancient, stone structures on Tyban IV, which suggest a civilization far older than Earth's, Steffens begins to question whether these beings are castaways or remnants of a long-vanished race. When he discovers the robots—perfectly engineered, intelligent, and seemingly alive—he realizes they may be the original inhabitants of the planet, preserved in metal to survive its harsh, radiation-filled environment. His decision to engage stems from a sense of duty: the League’s Contact Law forbids interaction with alien life, but Steffens recognizes that these beings are not mere machines—they are sentient, capable of communication and emotion. Moreover, he feels a moral obligation to investigate, as their existence challenges his assumptions about life, consciousness, and purpose. The robots’ clear desire to serve, their detailed knowledge of their own origins, and their silent, patient presence stir in Steffens a sense of wonder and responsibility. Ultimately, he chooses to land and engage not out of curiosity alone, but because he believes the encounter offers a rare opportunity to understand a civilization that has endured for millennia—possibly even longer than humanity—and that might hold answers about the nature of life, intelligence, and survival in the universe.
