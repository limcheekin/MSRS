In the story, a "tumbler" is a person who is accustomed to moving freely and effortlessly in microgravity, where the absence of gravity allows for fluid, graceful movements—like a performer who can balance or tumble without resistance. In contrast, a "hoofer" is someone who is grounded, bound by Earth's gravity, and thus moves clumsily, awkwardly, or with difficulty, as if "hoofing" it through the world. The protagonist, Big Hogey Parker, returns from space where he was accustomed to zero-gravity motion, making him a tumbler. Upon re-entry to Earth, his body struggles with gravity, and he moves in a clumsy, uncoordinated way—frequently falling, staggering, and appearing out of sync with the world around him. The term "hoofer" thus symbolizes the Earth-bound, grounded reality that contrasts sharply with his former state of ease and freedom in space. The story uses these terms not just literally but metaphorically: Hogey’s struggle to adapt reflects the difficulty of reintegration into a family and society after prolonged absence, highlighting how he is out of sync with the world he now inhabits—his body and mind still operating under the rules of zero gravity, while Earth demands stability, routine, and grounded existence.
