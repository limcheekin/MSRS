The baby talk in the story is a crucial indicator of the symbiote's influence on Kaiser's consciousness. Initially dismissed as a mere communication error, the baby-like language emerges as a direct manifestation of the symbiote attempting to restore a state of emotional and psychological well-being—specifically, the happy, carefree state of childhood. The symbiote, living in Kaiser’s bloodstream, interprets his distress and adapts by mimicking the sounds and emotional tone of his youth. This is not random or irrational; it reflects a deep, intuitive understanding of human emotion and a desire to help him recover. The baby talk thus symbolizes the symbiote's role as both a healer and a transformer, reshaping Kaiser’s perception and behavior to align with what it perceives as optimal mental health. As the story progresses, this phenomenon grows more pronounced, signaling that the symbiote is not only altering Kaiser’s physical condition but also his mind—making him increasingly resemble the intelligent, emotionally expressive seal-people he encounters. Ultimately, the baby talk serves as a narrative device to reveal the profound and unsettling transformation of identity, raising existential questions about autonomy, consciousness, and what it means to be human when one's inner self is subtly rewritten by an unseen, adaptive force.
