Herbert Quidley is a charismatic, observant, and somewhat naive man who becomes entangled in a secret romantic and social intrigue involving a clandestine interstellar society. He is not a member of the society but acts as an unwitting participant, drawn into their world through his discovery of coded messages hidden in a library copy of Taine’s *History of English Literature*. The messages, encoded with nonsense sequences like "asdf;lkj," are part of a secret communication system used by women from the planet Fieu Dayol, who face a gender imbalance and must seek mates on other worlds. 

Quidley's characteristics include his refined tastes—old books, wood, paintings, and wine—and his deep admiration for young, beautiful women, particularly those with a Helenesque grace. He is clever, perceptive, and resourceful, using tactics like "Operation Spill-the-sugar" to initiate contact with Kay Smith, a member of the society. However, he is also emotionally reactive and prone to overthinking, especially when confronted with the implications of the society's structure. Ultimately, he learns that Kay is not just a romantic interest but a key figure in an interstellar secret society, where "snoll dopers" are actual marriageable men selected for their refusal to conform to their home world’s sexual norms. Quidley’s journey transforms from romantic pursuit to realization of a complex, alien social system, making him both a participant and a reluctant observer in a world far beyond Earth.
