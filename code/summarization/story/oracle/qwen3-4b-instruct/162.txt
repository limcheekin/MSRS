The setting of *Master of Life and Death* is a distant, dystopian future—specifically, the 23rd century—where Earth's population has reached seven billion, leading to severe overpopulation and environmental strain. The story is primarily set within the Bureau of Population Equalization, commonly known as "Popeek," located in a towering, overdecorated building that reflects the decay of aesthetic values from the past. The Cullen Building, with its neo-Victorian excesses, symbolizes the bureaucratic and morally compromised nature of the society that has come to rely on extreme population control measures. Inside, the atmosphere is cold, sterile, and oppressive, emphasizing the dehumanizing function of the bureaucracy. The setting shifts between the office spaces, where reports and administrative tasks dominate, and the clinical environments of the euthanasia center, where decisions about life and death are carried out with chilling efficiency. This contrast underscores the story’s themes of technological control, moral ambiguity, and the erosion of human dignity in a society driven by survivalist logic. The urban backdrop—a bustling, overcrowded metropolis—further highlights the societal pressures that necessitate such drastic measures, grounding the futuristic setting in a recognizable human struggle for survival.
