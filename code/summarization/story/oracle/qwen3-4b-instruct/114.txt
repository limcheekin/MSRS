The meeting between Escher and MacDonald is prompted by a pressing colonization problem: the severe imbalance of gender ratios on Earth, where men are vastly outnumbered by women due to men's greater tendency to emigrate to other planets. This imbalance has led to social unrest, increased crime among women, puritanical laws, and a declining interest in emigration among men. The Colonization Board, seeking a solution, assigns Escher and MacDonald—responsible for practical policy implementation—to devise a plan to encourage women to colonize. MacDonald brings the problem directly to Escher, emphasizing the urgency and the consequences of inaction, such as worsening societal instability and a collapse in male emigration. Their meeting is thus a direct result of the Board’s need for a pragmatic, immediate solution to this systemic issue, with the goal of rethinking traditional colonial policies to make emigration attractive and feasible for women.
