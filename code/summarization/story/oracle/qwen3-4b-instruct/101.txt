Eric is both a victim and a central figure in the inhabitants' beliefs about the fantastical city. The people of the city—particularly the elders and the dreamers—view Eric not as a random visitor, but as a fulfillment of an ancient prophecy: "Eric the Bronze," destined to destroy the city. Though Eric initially resists this idea, he gradually realizes that he is not merely a victim of illusion, but a key element in the city’s self-sustaining mechanism. The city, a machine powered by collective thought, generates beauty and danger based on the people’s imaginations. When outsiders enter, their thoughts are absorbed and transmuted into reality, which causes instability—especially when individuals become consumed by greed or lust. Eric’s presence triggers this instability because he embodies the prophesied figure, whose arrival is meant to dismantle the machine. The inhabitants’ fear of him stems from their belief that he will destroy the city, but they also show deep respect for his role in restoring balance. Ultimately, Eric is both a threat and a necessary force in the city’s cycle—his relationship with the inhabitants is one of tension, prophecy, and reluctant destiny. He is not truly "real" in the way they are, yet he is central to their belief system, making him a symbolic bridge between the city’s illusion and its inevitable collapse.
