In the society described, others react with immediate discomfort, embarrassment, and polite confusion when the protagonist's name—his "unspeakable" four-letter designation—is mentioned. The name is deeply taboo because it violates the rigid, state-enforced nomenclature system, which maintains social order and conformity. When people hear the protagonist’s name, they experience a "crackling electric field" of unease; officials, such as supervisors or clerks, visibly react with redness, fidgeting, or avoidance. In practical settings, this manifests as rejection—such as store workers denying someone a package due to their "Non-Producer" status—or social exclusion, like being denied access to mating booths or social clubs. Even in professional environments, the mere mention of the name triggers hesitation and discomfort, with individuals avoiding direct interaction or quickly redirecting conversation. The protagonist’s name symbolizes nonconformity, and its recognition leads to immediate social distancing, reinforcing the state’s control through the power of naming. Thus, hearing the name is not just awkward—it is a provocation that triggers fear, shame, and institutional avoidance, demonstrating how deeply names are tied to social status and individual survival in this rigid society.
