The story revolves around a conformist named the narrator who lives in a tightly controlled society called Northem, where individuality is suppressed and identity is strictly regulated. Assigned a renumbered, "unspeakable" four-letter designation that causes social embarrassment and emotional distress, he begins to experience persistent dreams from a seductive woman whose voice urges him to defy the system. This inner conflict intensifies as he faces isolation, inability to mate, and rejection in social and institutional settings due to his non-conforming name. The narrative explores the psychological toll of conformity, showing how the state enforces uniformity through rigid naming systems, job assignments, and emotional regulation. The narrator's journey culminates in a pivotal encounter with a woman named Lara at the Govpub office, where he learns that changing his designation would require intervention from the Office of Psychological Adjustment (Opsych). Though he is discouraged by the bureaucratic barriers, the dream voice and Lara's warmth inspire him to pursue personal freedom. The plot ultimately centers on the struggle for identity and autonomy in a society that equates conformity with survival, making the act of seeking change both a personal and political rebellion.
