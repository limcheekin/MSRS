The newspapers are crucial to the story because they reveal the central mystery of time and identity. The girl, Mary Alice Pope, has lived in isolation on a remote island for nearly eighteen years, believing it is 1933, while the real year is 1953. The newspapers she receives—dated 1933, with yellow, brittle paper—are fabricated to maintain her false timeline and prevent her from realizing she has been living in a state of temporal isolation. Their presence confirms that her world is not grounded in reality but constructed by a deliberate deception. The dated headlines, such as "HITLER IN NEW DEFIANCE," and the radio broadcasts mimicking 1930s news further reinforce the illusion. When Jack discovers the newspapers are actually from 1933, it becomes clear that the boxes containing the notes and items are part of a scheme to keep her trapped in the past. The newspapers serve as tangible evidence of the manipulation, showing that her entire existence is a carefully orchestrated lie—proof that time itself has been distorted, and that the girl is not merely isolated, but deceived into believing she lives in a fabricated world. Thus, the newspapers are vital to exposing the truth about Mary Alice’s origins and the sinister plan to keep her trapped in a timeless, false reality.
