The setting of the story is the Lunar III outpost, a remote and isolated experimental dome on the Moon. This frontier station functions as a rocket refueling station, a teleradio transmission point, and a meteorological base. The environment is stark and artificial—characterized by constant temperature regulation, filtered sunlight, and reoxygenated air that lacks the natural freshness of Earth. Isobar Jones, the protagonist, lives in a confined, sterile space, cut off from the natural world and the comforts of home. The setting emphasizes isolation and psychological strain, as the outpost’s monotony and lack of freedom contribute to Isobar’s deep sense of homesickness and "world sickness." Despite the harsh conditions, the story contrasts the artificial interior with the vibrant, green, life-filled lunar valley outside, highlighting the emotional and physical longing for natural elements like grass, trees, and sunlight. The setting thus serves as both a physical backdrop and a symbolic representation of alienation, where the protagonist's journey to the outside world becomes a quest for emotional and existential relief.
