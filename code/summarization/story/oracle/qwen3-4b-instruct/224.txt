The setting of the story is the harsh, desolate Great Plains of Earth, a treeless, barren landscape marked by rolling hills, dusty terrain, and an omnipresent yellow dust that gives the environment a surreal, almost otherworldly quality. The land is both physically unforgiving and emotionally isolating, emphasizing the protagonist’s alienation upon returning home. This arid, monotonous expanse mirrors the internal turmoil of Big Hogey Parker, who struggles with physical disorientation due to his time in space and emotional instability stemming from his absence. The setting serves as a metaphor for the challenges of reintegration into family life—where the familiar seems foreign, and the simple act of returning home feels like a journey into chaos. The contrast between the vibrant, alien world of "Big Bottomless" (a space station or deep-space environment) and the mundane, grounded reality of Earth underscores the theme of dislocation. The sun and moon are described in distorted, phony terms, reflecting Hogey’s impaired perception and psychological state. Ultimately, the setting amplifies the story’s central conflict: a man who was once a skilled "tumbler" in space finds himself unable to adapt to the gravity, responsibilities, and emotional demands of domestic life, making the return home not just a physical journey but a profound personal failure.
