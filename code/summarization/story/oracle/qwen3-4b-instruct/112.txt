Some odd things that happened in the story include the mysterious arrival of women from Earth being paired with men from Midplanet through a marriage draft, despite neither side being willing to leave their world. The women were presented as glamorous and sophisticated, yet the narrative reveals their isolation and frustration—such as Phyllis Hanson’s anxiety over unfulfilled marital prospects and her eventual decision to emigrate after being offered a choice between jail or colonization. Another odd element is the sudden, seemingly out-of-place promotional poster advertising "the Planets of Romance," which depicts a man on a frontier planet and openly appeals to women's desire for adventure and escape. This poster, which Phyllis initially believes violates her privacy, is later revealed to be part of a larger campaign to encourage women to colonize. The most bizarre twist is the offer made by the Colonization Board: instead of just sending women to planets, they offer them a choice between ten years in prison and a $10,000 fine—or emigration with a $500 bonus, framed as a solution to a gender imbalance. This stark, almost coercive incentive, where personal freedom is traded for a chance at a new life, underscores the story's odd and unsettling premise of using forced migration to resolve social imbalances.
