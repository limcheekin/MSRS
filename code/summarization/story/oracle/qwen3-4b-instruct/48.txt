The narrator’s name, which is "unspeakable" and a four-letter designation forbidden in the society, profoundly isolates him socially. Because his name violates the state’s rigid nomenclature rules, he is systematically rejected in all social and professional settings. When others hear his name, they experience immediate discomfort, confusion, or embarrassment—leading to avoidance. This prevents him from joining groups, clubs, or even State Loyalty organizations. Most critically, his name makes him ineligible for mating, as Eugenic Centers refuse to allow contact with individuals bearing such a designation. The societal structure ensures that his name is not just a label but a tool of exclusion, making personal relationships and social integration impossible. Even in moments of intimacy—such as the brief physical encounter with Lara—he feels exposed and vulnerable, knowing that any deviation from conformity risks severe punishment. Thus, his name becomes a symbol of alienation, driving deep loneliness and reinforcing the state’s control over identity, emotion, and human connection.
