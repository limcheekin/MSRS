Sergeant Andy McCloud navigates a chaotic situation involving a mysterious epidemic by taking charge of a deteriorating crisis marked by military bureaucracy and confusion. Initially dismissed and relieved of his duties by higher-ranking officers who view his rank as insufficient, Andy defies the system by insisting on autonomy and immediate action. He works tirelessly with Corporal Bettijean to gather data, identify patterns, and uncover the root cause of the illness. Through meticulous analysis of victim demographics—such as writers, poets, and office workers—Andy deduces that the outbreak is linked to licking four-cent stamps, a habit common among individuals in small offices or creative fields. The solution hinges on recognizing the American practice of stamp-licking, which is absent in government workers, postal clerks (who use sponges), and other professionals. Ultimately, Andy successfully identifies the culprit—a contaminated adhesive—and recommends public warnings and investigations into stamp manufacturing security. Despite being undermined by brass officers, he emerges as a capable, determined leader who outsmarts bureaucratic inertia with practical insight, intelligence, and resilience.
