The siren in the story serves as a crucial symbol of both danger and communication, highlighting the perilous relationship between the explorers and the native inhabitants. Initially, it functions as a defensive mechanism—its loud wail scares off an impending attack by warriors hidden among moving sponge-trees, demonstrating the immediate threat posed by the strange, hostile environment. However, its significance extends beyond mere survival; the natives interpret the siren’s sound as a protective signal, echoing their own language of "see-o-see-o," which they use to convey safety and ward off further attacks. This reflects their deep cultural connection to the rhythms of their world—the Serpent River (the "Kao-Wagwattl") is not just a physical feature but a living, sacred force that shapes their beliefs and behaviors. The siren, therefore, becomes a bridge between the alien world and the explorers’ understanding of its logic. Its persistent sound symbolizes the tension between human technology and native intuition, showing that true understanding requires more than observation—it demands respect for the natural and spiritual order of the planet. In this way, the siren underscores the central theme: the Serpent River is not just a geographical phenomenon but a living entity that governs life, conflict, and cultural survival on this world.
