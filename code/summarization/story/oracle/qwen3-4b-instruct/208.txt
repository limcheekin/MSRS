Mackenzie is a psychiatrist who works with Martin Isherwood, the man driven by an ambition to reach the moon. He is tasked by the Air Force with evaluating Isherwood’s mental state and psychological readiness for a rocket mission. Mackenzie is portrayed as a cautious and deeply concerned professional, recognizing that Isherwood’s intense focus on becoming a rocket pilot has isolated him from personal relationships, emotions, and human connections. He warns that Isherwood’s single-minded dedication—having no family, friends, or interests beyond rocketry—makes him vulnerable to psychological breakdown. Mackenzie hypnotizes Isherwood during a pre-mission evaluation, implanting a hallucination that Isherwood has already been to the Moon. This is intended to prevent the emotional and psychological intensity of the real mission from becoming overwhelming. However, the hallucination ultimately diminishes the adventure and meaning of the actual flight, which Isherwood later realizes. Mackenzie’s intervention fails in its goal, as Isherwood still proceeds on the mission, eventually dying on the Moon while mourning his lost sense of purpose. Mackenzie's actions highlight the story's central theme: that true human ambition must be balanced with emotional and personal connection to survive.
