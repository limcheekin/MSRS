Eric finds himself in a city on Mars that is characterized by its strange beauty and underlying danger. This city is not a natural settlement but a massive machine built from the collective imagination of its people. It appears as a fantastical, jewel-like metropolis with spires and minarets that twinkle under the Martian sky, emitting a haunting, seductive music that draws visitors in. The city’s beauty is deeply alluring, promising wonders like fur-covered streets, wine, and women of springtime grace. However, this beauty is an illusion created by a machine that translates thought into reality. The city is dangerous because it manipulates human perception—inducing hallucinations, instilling obsession, and corrupting minds. It feeds on desires, transforming them into physical forms, leading to madness and loss of self. Visitors who enter become vulnerable to psychological possession, as seen when Eric is initially drawn in and then transformed into a feared figure, "Eric the Bronze," a legend of destruction. The city’s true nature emerges as a self-sustaining machine that grows with collective thought, but becomes unstable when outsiders interfere. Its danger lies not just in its physical form, but in its power to override reality and erode free will, making it both mesmerizing and perilous.
