In the context of the story, "pre-civilization" refers to a state of existence characterized by raw, unrefined human nature—marked by instinct, violence, chaos, and a lack of societal restraint. The Butcher, a young boy who believes in the necessity of warfare, envisions pre-civilization as a time when people were constantly hurting each other, before humanity developed systems of peace, control, and emotional regulation through conditioning. This concept is embodied in the Time Theater's depiction of ancient warriors and a hooded sorcerer, representing a world where violence, fear, and primal instincts dominate. The Butcher's fascination with such scenes reflects his belief that true human nature is violent and that civilization’s suppression of violence is an artificial construct. For him, pre-civilization is not a historical stage but a psychological state—deeply rooted in childhood impulses and the desire for bloodshed, which he sees as the authentic, unmediated form of human behavior. This contrasts sharply with the futuristic society where children are conditioned to reject violence through structured programs, making "pre-civilization" a symbol of primal freedom and chaos, which the story presents as both terrifying and fundamentally human.
