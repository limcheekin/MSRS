The plot centers on Jack Barry, a biology student who sails to a secluded island and encounters a mysterious girl named Mary Alice Pope. The girl lives in isolation with her aunts, claiming to have been born during World War II and living in 1933, despite the year being 1953. She receives mysterious boxes containing notes, poems, and other items signed only "Your Lover," which she believes are love letters from a romantic figure. As Jack investigates, he discovers that the newspaper, radio broadcasts, and the girl's timeline are all from 1933, indicating a profound temporal or psychological dislocation. The revelation comes when Mrs. Kesserich, Martin Kesserich’s wife, reveals that Mary Alice was actually Martin’s fiancée who died in a railway accident in 1933. The island is a time-locked or memory-based prison where the girl has been living in a fabricated reality, isolated from the world and time. Jack realizes that the "boxes" are not from a lover but from Martin Kesserich himself, sent as a way to maintain her illusion of life and love. The story explores themes of identity, time, memory, and the manipulation of perception, culminating in the unsettling truth that Mary Alice is a ghostly echo of a woman who died decades ago—her existence preserved in a fabricated reality by her grieving fiancé, who uses time and memory to keep her alive in an eternal, isolated world.
