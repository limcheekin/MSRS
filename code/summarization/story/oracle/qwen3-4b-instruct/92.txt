Martin Kesserich is a renowned biologist and the central figure in the story, known for his profound dedication to science and his deep, all-consuming love for Mary Alice Pope. He is described as a man of intense emotional passion, particularly in his love for Mary, which shapes his life and research. In his past, Martin fell deeply in love with Mary Alice Pope, a young woman of British descent with a sharp intelligence and warmth. Their relationship was tragically cut short when she was killed in a railway accident in 1933, an event that devastated him. Despite this loss, Martin never moved on—his life became a perpetual memorial to her, with meticulous plans for their future unfolding year by year. He built a life centered on her memory, including detailed intentions for their travels and shared experiences. The story reveals that he married Mrs. Kesserich, a woman who serves as a foil to his emotional depth, but his true love remains Mary Alice. The mysterious girl, Mary Alice Pope, who lives on the isolated island, is actually a time-locked duplicate of the real Mary Alice, kept in a state of perpetual 1933 through a psychological and possibly supernatural manipulation orchestrated by Martin’s own obsessive devotion. Thus, Martin Kesserich is not only a brilliant scientist but also a man consumed by love, grief, and a haunting belief that he can preserve Mary Alice's life through time and illusion.
