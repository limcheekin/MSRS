The society in *Time in the Round* is structured around a futuristic, highly technologically advanced civilization where children are systematically conditioned to reject violence through early exposure to non-harmful simulations and "uninjes"—programmed, uninjurable robotic animals. These uninjes serve as emotional and psychological tools, allowing children to experience conflict and aggression without real physical consequences. The society emphasizes pacification and emotional regulation, with institutions like the Time Theater designed to show historical violence only in a passive, observational manner, reinforcing the idea that violence is unnatural and should be avoided. Children are taught that real-world conflict is a failure of emotional maturity, and that peaceful, automated solutions are superior. However, the story reveals a deep contradiction: despite this conditioning, the protagonist, the "Butcher," harbors a visceral, pre-civilization desire for violence, believing that true civilization must include warfare. This internal conflict suggests that the society's structure is fragile, relying on technological suppression of instinct rather than genuine moral or emotional development. The setting thus reflects a society that has achieved peace through artificial control, where the very possibility of real violence—such as time-traveling to alter the past—is feared and strictly forbidden, revealing a civilization that may be emotionally sterile and psychologically constrained.
