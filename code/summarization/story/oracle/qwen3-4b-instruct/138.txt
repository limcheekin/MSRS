The President's communications to the public are deliberately deceptive and emotionally manipulative, designed to preserve societal stability. Upon learning the truth from Michael and Mary—namely, that Earth is dead, all other planets are uninhabitable, and humanity has failed catastrophically through neglect and violence—the President chooses not to reveal the full horror. Instead, he alters their report, presenting a fabricated narrative of hope: a lush, green planet found in space, with promises of salvation and future migration. This lie is rooted in the fear that showing the truth—of violent deaths, barren desolation, and human extinction—would trigger mass hysteria and mental breakdowns among the population, who have been conditioned for centuries to avoid the sight of violent death.

The President justifies this deception by claiming that the people’s sanity must be protected, arguing that their inability to endure the reality of death or destruction would lead to societal collapse. He ultimately orders Michael and Mary to be isolated, their truth suppressed, and a new expedition sent to find another planet. However, when Michael and Mary refuse to be silenced and instead demand to leave Earth and live a natural, grounded life, the President is forced to confront the moral weight of his decision. Though he initially resists, he ultimately agrees to let them go, recognizing that their choice—living out a natural, quiet life on the dying Earth—is a form of truth-telling that, paradoxically, offers hope. Thus, the President’s communications are not merely a failure of honesty, but a calculated act of social preservation, ultimately undermined by the emotional and moral strength of the couple’s decision to live and die authentically in the face of ruin.
