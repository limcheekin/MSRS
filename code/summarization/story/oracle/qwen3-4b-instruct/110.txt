The story explores the plot of men and women from different worlds navigating the complexities of relationships and societal expectations through a sci-fi colonization scenario. On one planet, men have colonized distant worlds while women remained on Earth, creating a severe gender imbalance. This imbalance leads to social issues like increased crime, puritanical laws, and psychological distress among unmarried women. To solve this, the Colonization Board proposes sending Earth women to the colony planets, where they can form families and balance the gender ratio. The narrative follows several characters—from Karl Allen, who is eager for a wife from Earth, to Phyllis Hanson, who initially resists but is drawn in by the promise of a new life—highlighting the emotional and societal struggles of women facing loneliness and unmet expectations. The story reveals how societal pressures, such as the lack of marriage opportunities, drive women to consider emigration, even when it seems like a radical departure from their lives. Ultimately, the plot centers on the tension between traditional gender roles and the desire for personal freedom, as individuals confront the possibility of love, identity, and belonging across worlds. The solution is not just logistical but deeply human—offering a new kind of relationship built on equality, adventure, and mutual survival.
