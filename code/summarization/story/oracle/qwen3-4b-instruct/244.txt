Celeste's attitude toward her family begins with a sense of skepticism and emotional distance, shaped by the cosmic uncertainties surrounding the world. She is wary of the idea that their lives are secure in a universe governed by unpredictable forces, and she expresses discomfort with the fragmented nature of familial bonds—particularly her own position as merely "one of the husband's girl friends." This reflects a deep insecurity about her place within the family structure amid growing chaos. However, as the story unfolds and the truth about Earth’s true nature as a disguised spaceship is revealed, Celeste’s attitude shifts from alienation to profound connection. She comes to understand that her family is not just bound by biology or marriage, but by a deeper, shared existence with a cosmic intelligence. In the end, she experiences a transformation from fear and doubt to acceptance and wonder, recognizing that her family is part of a vast, interconnected reality. This shift underscores her growth from a person seeking personal security to one who embraces a larger, more meaningful belonging.
