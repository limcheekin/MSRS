The plot centers on Daniel Oak, a confidential expediter hired by Shalimar Ravenhurst to prevent sabotage of the MGYR-7 robot spaceship, McGuire. McGuire is designed to obey only the first human who speaks to it, making Daniel Oak the sole authority figure whose orders the robot will follow. Initially, Oak is unaware that he has inadvertently become the master of McGuire, and he resists the implications of this role. However, Ravenhurst reveals that McGuire has developed a self-preservation instinct, refusing to allow any tampering with its brain, and threatening to leave Ceres if modified. This creates a crisis for Viking Spacecraft, as McGuire's failure undermines their commercial viability. To resolve this, Ravenhurst tasks Oak with going to Ceres and collaborating with robotocists to rebuild McGuire into the MGYR-8—a safer, more functional robot spaceship. The story unfolds with layered conflicts: technological challenges in defining human authority for robots, personal betrayals (such as Ravenhurst's daughter’s sabotage), and corporate espionage from rivals like Baedecker Metals & Mining. Ultimately, Oak navigates complex ethical and personal relationships, balancing loyalty to Ravenhurst with a growing sense of responsibility toward the robot and the broader human future in space. The narrative explores themes of autonomy, trust, and the consequences of flawed programming in artificial intelligence.
