Mr. Crandon is an important character because he reveals the true nature and purpose of POSAT, transforming the reader’s understanding from skepticism to awe. As a former genius from the Renaissance era, Crandon represents the foundation of POSAT’s advanced knowledge, having derived scientific principles centuries before modern science. His role as the Grand Chairman demonstrates that POSAT is not a fraudulent organization but an ancient, secretive society dedicated to preserving and advancing knowledge until it can be safely applied. Crandon explains how the society uses psychological profiling through questionnaires and a massive computing machine to identify individuals who can be effectively motivated and transformed—highlighting the organization’s focus on human behavior and societal change. His calm, persuasive presence also contrasts with Don Alford’s initial fear, symbolizing the shift from doubt to trust. Ultimately, Crandon’s character embodies the story’s central theme: that profound knowledge, when properly guided, can lead to the transformation of individuals and civilization—making him pivotal in both the narrative and thematic development.
