Tony Carmen is a Mafia-connected racketeer who becomes the central figure in the narrative after approaching Professor Venetti, a government scientist, to use his invention—a device capable of destroying matter without producing energy—to dispose of bodies. Initially, Carmen claims he needs the machine to eliminate corpses left at his club by gangsters, asserting they are accident victims rather than murder victims. However, he soon reveals that he plans to use the device as a tool for criminal operations, launching a broader campaign known as the "Expendables," which allows him to eliminate enemies without leaving physical evidence. He leverages his business acumen and stock ownership in various corporations to promote and commercialize the invention, leaking information to manipulate public perception and attract corporate interest, particularly from Arcivox, which seeks innovative appliances. Despite Venetti’s scientific and ethical reservations, Carmen ultimately takes control of the machine’s use, deploying it to destroy individuals—such as Harry Keno and his associates—without a body to prove the crime (no *corpus delecti*). The narrative unfolds with escalating tension as government agents, specifically from the Atomic Energy Commission (AEC), investigate the invention due to its apparent violation of the conservation of energy. The AEC discovers that the machine converts matter into heat, contributing to rising global temperatures and threatens planetary stability. Though Carmen initially exploits the device for criminal gain, the story ultimately reveals his actions as a calculated, dangerous misuse of science, setting up a moral and scientific conflict between innovation, control, and unintended consequences.
