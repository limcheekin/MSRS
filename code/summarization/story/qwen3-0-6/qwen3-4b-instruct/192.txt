The theme of marriage in *Prime Difference* is explored through the protagonist’s desperate struggle with his wife Marge, ultimately revealing that marriage is not about permanence or emotional compatibility, but about control and illusion. The man’s initial frustration with Marge—her constant complaints, emotional volatility, and sense of being "permanently" married—leads him to seek a solution: replacing her with a duplicate, George Prime. This act symbolizes a deeper disillusionment with marriage as a binding, unchangeable institution. However, the story subverts expectations by showing that Marge never truly left; she was always aware of the deception and orchestrated the situation to maintain emotional intimacy while preserving the illusion of a genuine relationship. The resolution reveals that the man’s fear of loss and instability stems from a personal failure to see his wife as she truly is—sly, intelligent, and emotionally resilient. Ultimately, marriage is not about perfection or replacement, but about mutual understanding and the courage to face emotional truth. The story critiques societal expectations of marriage by exposing how both partners can be complicit in maintaining false narratives, and how true connection requires vulnerability, not technological substitution.
