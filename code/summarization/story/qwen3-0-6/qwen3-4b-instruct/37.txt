The siren holds profound significance in the explorers' encounter with the strange inhabitants, serving as both a physical and symbolic tool of communication and survival. When the explorers' siren wails, it triggers an immediate and dramatic response from the hostile warrior group, causing them to break their attack formation and retreat. This reaction reveals that the siren is not merely a mechanical device but a signal deeply embedded in the local environment’s perception of danger. The inhabitants—specifically the people of Tomboldo—recognize the siren’s sound as a warning, echoing the phrase "see-o-see-o," which they use to indicate safety. This demonstrates that the siren functions as a shared cultural or environmental signal, one that the native population has either learned to interpret or has been exposed to through prior interactions. Its power lies in its ability to disrupt aggression and restore order, highlighting its role as a mediator between hostile forces and peace. Moreover, the siren’s failure when Gravgak is struck by a stone from a club underscores its vulnerability and the danger of misinterpretation—showing that while it can calm the inhabitants, it cannot protect against physical threats. Ultimately, the siren symbolizes the fragile balance between human technology and alien consciousness, emphasizing how communication, even through simple sounds, can shape the outcome of encounters in unfamiliar and perilous surroundings.
