The "chicken colonel" in the story is a young, rigid military officer who represents bureaucratic rigidity and authority. Throughout the narrative, he initially acts with fear and frustration, especially when confronted by Sergeant Major Andy McCloud, a non-commissioned officer who takes charge during a mysterious epidemic. The colonel is deeply offended by Andy’s position, calling him a "noncom" and dismissing his leadership as "irregular." He attempts to assert control by demanding that Andy and Bettijean be relieved of their duties and face disciplinary action. However, his authority is undermined when the general, recognizing the gravity of the situation, overrides his orders and appoints Andy and Bettijean as the de facto leaders of the response. Ultimately, the colonel is sidelined, and his resistance ends in failure. His presence highlights the contrast between rigid military protocol and practical, on-the-ground leadership. By the end, he is no longer in control, and the story shows that the real solution lies in adaptability, not hierarchy.
