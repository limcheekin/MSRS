June feels a mix of wonder, curiosity, and emotional warmth toward Patrick Mead. Upon first meeting him, she is struck by his striking resemblance to herself—his red hair, tall frame, and tanned appearance—prompting her to reflect on the idea that people on Minos may all look alike due to shared ancestry. This realization brings both surprise and a sense of connection. June senses his vitality and authenticity, which contrasts sharply with the artificiality of shipboard life. She is drawn to his grounded, frontier spirit and his open, unguarded demeanor. When he touches her arm, she responds with light humor, acknowledging his presence as a hero of the moment. Though initially cautious, June grows fond of him, appreciating his simple honesty and the depth of his history—especially the plague that shaped his people’s survival and their unique appearance. His story of the “melting sickness” and the Mead family’s immunity deepens her empathy. June sees in Patrick not just a stranger from a distant colony, but a human being whose life is rooted in resilience, tradition, and genuine connection—qualities that make him both compelling and comforting.
