The seal-people in the narrative are significant as a symbol of survival and adaptation in an alien environment. They represent a species that has evolved through symbiotic relationships with a virus, enabling them to endure extreme environmental conditions such as radical weather cycles. This biological adaptation demonstrates how life can transform in response to harsh conditions, mirroring the broader theme of survival in alien ecosystems. Their presence on Tintera, particularly in a riverine settlement, shows not only physical resilience but also cultural development—more advanced than the first encounter, indicating that adaptation is not just biological but also social and technological. The interaction between Kaiser and the seal-people reveals their ability to communicate and establish peaceful contact, suggesting that survival is intertwined with intelligence and cooperation. Furthermore, their existence validates the hypothesis that alien life may thrive through symbiosis rather than direct evolution, reinforcing the narrative's exploration of how species adapt to survive beyond Earth-like conditions. Thus, the seal-people serve as a central example of how life can evolve and persist in hostile, unfamiliar environments through biological and cultural innovation.
