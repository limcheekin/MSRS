The story involves a man, John Smith, grappling with unsettling memories and a distorted reality shaped by personal loss and mysterious, unexplained phenomena. In the narrative, John experiences a terrifying breakdown after consuming alcohol, which amplifies his perception of reality. He begins to perceive his own body as filled with "holes," a disturbing realization that suggests a disintegration of physical and mental integrity. This psychological unraveling is compounded by vivid, fragmented memories—such as the disappearance of his childhood friend Davie and the mistaken belief that he had a son—indicating a profound loss of reality. The story unfolds in a surreal, dreamlike atmosphere where time, memory, and identity blur. John’s descent into madness is exacerbated by environmental anomalies, such as the sudden appearance and disappearance of figures in the streets, and the eerie silence of a world governed by unspoken rules. His frantic attempts to find answers—by investigating strange regulations, missing people, and unexplained events—highlight a deeper existential crisis. Ultimately, the plot centers on John’s struggle to reconcile his fractured memories with a world that no longer reflects his truth, making his isolation and psychological collapse a direct result of both personal loss and a reality warped by unseen forces.
