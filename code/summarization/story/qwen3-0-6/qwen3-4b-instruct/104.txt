The significance of Garve leaving the ship and following the mysterious allure of an enchanting yet perilous city lies in his symbolic journey from survival to sacrifice. Though the narrative does not explicitly name a city, the reference to "an enchanting yet perilous city" aligns with the story's thematic undercurrents—particularly in the final chapter where Steve and Mary reach the caravan and are assured of safety, while Garve's absence suggests he has chosen a path of personal destiny over communal safety. His departure mirrors the motif of individuals drawn to mysterious, possibly illusory or dangerous destinations, reflecting deeper human yearnings for meaning, adventure, or connection beyond survival. This choice underscores the story’s exploration of moral and emotional complexity: Garve, like the characters in the other stories, is not merely reacting to external threats but being pulled by internal forces—perhaps a desire for redemption, truth, or personal transformation. His journey into the unknown, despite its peril, embodies the courage to pursue something greater than mere survival, highlighting the tension between safety and self-determination. In this way, Garve’s departure becomes a powerful commentary on the human spirit's persistent pull toward mystery and purpose.
