The theme of capitalism is explored through the intersection of scientific innovation, profit-driven motives, and the exploitation of societal systems. In the story involving the scientist and the gangster, the revolutionary waste disposal invention symbolizes a powerful technological tool that could solve environmental problems. However, instead of being used for public good, it is commodified and seized by a gangster who uses it to eliminate bodies—highlighting how capitalism prioritizes profit over ethical responsibility. The scientist’s initial moral revulsion at using government-funded research for illegal purposes reflects the tension between scientific advancement and capitalist exploitation. This is further reinforced by Trader Tom’s "Trader Tom plan," where purchases are made without actual payment, charges are deducted from an estate, and there are no guarantees—exposing the absurdity and dehumanizing nature of consumer capitalism. The invention becomes a commercial product, marketed to children with exaggerated, aggressive imagery, demonstrating how capitalist systems manipulate values and desires for profit. Ultimately, the story critiques capitalism’s tendency to reduce complex ethical issues—like waste disposal or human dignity—to transactional exchanges, revealing its capacity to corrupt both science and morality.
