Hal is a young boy in a futuristic story involving children and a controversial perspective on violence. He serves as a guide and moral anchor for the other children, particularly in discussions about war and violence. In the narrative, Hal explains that violent behavior in young people is a natural part of development and that societies eventually condition individuals to reject violence—especially when dealing with non-human threats. He argues that adults are taught to avoid violent solutions to problems, citing historical examples like wars and revolutions, and emphasizes that this shift comes through emotional and psychological conditioning. Hal’s role is both educational and reassuring, as he gently counters the Butcher’s strong belief that warfare will persist in the future. He believes that by age six, children will have matured emotionally and will no longer view violence as a natural or acceptable response. Thus, Hal embodies a rational, forward-thinking perspective on peace and human development, contrasting with the Butcher’s defiant stance, and underscores the story’s central theme: that violent tendencies are not inherent but can be overcome through growth and education.
