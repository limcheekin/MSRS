The significance of the army in a story about a peaceful colony facing an unexpected threat lies in its role as a symbol of preparedness, authority, and the necessary force to enforce order and survival. In this narrative, the army represents a hidden but essential presence—despite the colony's pacifist ideals and daily tranquility. The characters’ initial disbelief in the fleet’s strength or military readiness underscores how peace has masked vulnerability. When the alien threat emerges, the army’s absence is revealed as a failure of trust and planning. The characters' resistance to the idea of military action—such as the colonists’ complaints about the lack of protection—highlights the tension between civilian pacifism and the reality of existential danger. Yet, the army’s eventual involvement, particularly through figures like Dylan and Extrone, signals a shift from passive survival to active defense. The army's preparation, even if covert or long-term, reflects a strategic foresight that the threat is not merely external but also deeply systemic. Thus, the army serves as both a literal force and a metaphor for the necessary institutional strength required to protect a society when peace is shattered by an unforeseen crisis.
