The story explores the return of a man and woman, Michael and Mary, to a desolate Earth after a two-thousand-year journey through space. Upon arriving, they discover that Earth has been utterly destroyed—cracked, burned, and reduced to a barren cinder, with cities now mere ruins circling shrinking seas. The planet, once lush and vibrant, is now a wasteland, a stark contrast to the idealized vision of Earth they once held. Though their mission was to find a habitable world, they instead uncover a tragic failure: humanity’s neglect and self-destruction led to Earth’s demise. Michael, hardened by time and space, believes no life can survive on the planet, while Mary, emotionally devastated, longs for the natural beauty and freedom of Earth. Their emotional confrontation reveals deep personal and existential despair. Ultimately, they choose to return to Earth—not to rebuild, but to witness its ruin firsthand, knowing that humanity’s future lies not in survival on this broken world, but in a new beginning beyond it. The story serves as a cautionary tale about environmental collapse, human hubris, and the fragility of civilization.
