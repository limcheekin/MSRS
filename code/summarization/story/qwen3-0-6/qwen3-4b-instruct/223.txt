In the story, a "tumbler" is a man who is accustomed to moving freely and effortlessly in zero gravity, where body movements are fluid and unimpeded—like a person who once performed acrobatic feats in space. A "hoofer," in contrast, is someone who has adapted to Earth's gravity and now moves awkwardly, struggling with balance and coordination, as if walking on land with stiff, clumsy legs. The protagonist, Big Hogey Parker, describes himself as a "tumbler" because he was used to the weightlessness of space, where he could move with ease and grace. After returning to Earth, he finds that gravity has "clutched" him and that his legs no longer work properly—his movements are uncoordinated, and he stumbles frequently. This contrast between tumbler and hoofer symbolizes the disorientation and physical struggle of re-adapting to a world with gravity after prolonged exposure to zero-G. The story uses these terms not just literally but metaphorically to express the emotional and psychological toll of returning to Earth—where one's former abilities are now undermined by the very force that once seemed alien.
