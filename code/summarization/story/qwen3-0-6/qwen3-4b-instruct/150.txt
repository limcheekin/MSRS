The plot centers on archaeologist Michaelson, who discovers a mysterious ancient city hidden in a desert. Upon arrival, he is confronted by a native named Maota, who claims to be the "keeper of the city" and insists that no outsiders should enter. Maota warns that the spirits of the dead dwell within the ruins and that any intrusion—especially by an alien voice—could awaken them and cause catastrophic consequences. Michaelson, however, is captivated by the city’s beauty and historical significance, believing it to be a priceless archaeological treasure. He attempts to collect artifacts, including ancient books, bowls, and statues, and persuades Maota to help preserve the site by building a museum. But Maota refuses, fearing that preserving the city for future study would disrupt its spiritual balance and invite the spirits back to life. In a dramatic climax, Maota strikes Michaelson with an ancient book, which rustles as if reading aloud, causing a head wound. The encounter ends with Michaelson standing alone in the ruins, deeply affected by the city’s age and the tension between scientific curiosity and spiritual reverence. The story explores themes of cultural preservation, the danger of human intrusion into sacred spaces, and the conflict between progress and tradition.
