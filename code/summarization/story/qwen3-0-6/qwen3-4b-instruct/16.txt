Sergeant Andy McCloud navigates a chaotic situation as the mysterious "Red Plague" spreads across the United States, triggering panic and military confusion. Initially dismissed by higher-ranking officers due to his rank as a non-commissioned officer, Andy takes charge of the crisis in a Pentagon office where chaos and bureaucratic inertia dominate. Despite being overwhelmed by fear, exhaustion, and constant interruptions, he remains focused and resourceful. He investigates patterns in who is infected—finding that writers, artists, poets, and young office workers are affected, while doctors, dentists, postal workers, and military personnel remain unaffected. Through careful observation and logical deduction, Andy realizes the key lies in a seemingly minor detail: licking stamps. The outbreak is not contagious but is linked to the habit of licking stamps, which is common among writers, artists, and people in small offices. He discovers that a four-cent stamp found in a victim’s possession is the critical clue. With this insight, Andy convinces the military leadership to issue a public warning against licking stamps, exposing the false alarm as a case of mistaken causation. Ultimately, Andy defuses the crisis with intelligence, perseverance, and a touch of humor, earning respect not just from his team but from the general public and high-ranking officials.
