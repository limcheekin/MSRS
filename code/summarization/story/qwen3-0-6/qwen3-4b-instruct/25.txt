The plot of *Time in the Round* by Fritz Leiber centers on a young boy named the Butcher, who is five years old and deeply resistant to the idea of peace and civilization. Set in a futuristic world filled with peculiar creatures—such as uninj dogs made of circuits and hyperplastic materials—the story explores the boy’s struggle with violence and his belief that warfare is inevitable, even when he is told it is a childish notion. The narrative unfolds as the trio of boys—Butcher, Hal, and Joggy—approach the mysterious Time Theater, a towering structure that displays historical events but is off-limits to children under age six. The Butcher refuses to accept the rules, insisting he will have warfare when he becomes World Director, and challenges the system by attempting to enter the Theater despite being barred. This defiance reflects his internal conflict between youthful aggression and the societal conditioning meant to foster peace. The story also introduces themes of fear, control, and the dangers of unchecked violence, as the Butcher’s actions—like his violent experiment on the dog Brute—reveal a raw, unrefined understanding of power. Ultimately, the plot illustrates how young minds grapple with complex moral and philosophical ideas, and how civilization attempts to suppress violent impulses through education and structure. The story ends with the Butcher reluctantly accepting the rules, symbolizing the gradual, often painful, process of maturation and acceptance of societal norms.
