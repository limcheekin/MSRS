The narrator’s name, Kevin O'Malley, carries significant social consequences in the highly controlled society depicted in the story. As a non-Producer (N/P) with no official post, the narrator is deemed undesirable and powerless in the eyes of the state. His name, which is legally registered and unchangeable, directly limits his access to social and economic opportunities. In the system where identity is strictly regulated, individuals like Kevin are treated as drones—drains on the state—because their names are tied to non-productive status. This prevents him from joining social clubs, participating in group activities, or even forming relationships. The society’s rigid hierarchy assigns value based on one’s designation, and since "Kevin" is linked to N/P status, he is systematically excluded from normal social interactions. His attempt to use his name in a personal context—such as when speaking to the attractive girl at the information desk—triggers immediate suspicion and discomfort, revealing how deeply identity shapes social exclusion. The narrator’s name, therefore, is not just a label but a legal and social barrier that defines his marginalization and reinforces the state’s control over individual lives.
