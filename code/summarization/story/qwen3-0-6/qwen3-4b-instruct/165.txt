The story involving a spaceman's adventures and a deadly lure in the void of space is *The Hoofer* by Walter M. Miller, Jr. The plot centers on Ben, a young man who has dreamed of deep space exploration but returns to Earth after a personal crisis, seeking connection and meaning. He is drawn into a haunting experience when he encounters a tri-dimensional photo of a red-bearded spaceman, which becomes a powerful psychological anchor. This figure, later revealed to be his deceased husband, Maggie, symbolizes both a lost love and a dangerous lure in the void—representing the emotional and existential risks of space travel. The red-bearded man, though dead, remains a force in Ben's mind, manifesting in visions and dreams, suggesting that the "deadly lure" is not physical but psychological—fear, loss, and the haunting presence of those left behind in space. As Ben grapples with grief and identity, he learns that the true danger lies not in the emptiness of space, but in the emotional void it can leave behind. The story ultimately explores how space travel isolates individuals from human connection, making the memory of loved ones a dangerous, alluring, and ultimately devastating force in the silence of the void.
