The setting of the story revolves around Steve Cantwell’s return to his abandoned, desolate village on Sirius, a harsh and barren world. The environment is overwhelmingly hostile—searing desert heat, parching winds, and a pervasive sense of isolation. Once a vibrant community, the village now exists as a ghost town, with only the crumbling shells of mud-brick houses remaining. The land itself is poisoned, as evidenced by the bitter, toxic water from the central well, which had been poisoned shortly before Steve’s arrival. This physical decay mirrors the emotional and psychological devastation of the place. The atmosphere is one of silence, abandonment, and creeping dread, broken only by the presence of a withered old Kumaji, who lives on the edge of society and speaks of a world lost to violence and displacement. The setting functions as both a literal and symbolic representation of loss—of family, of innocence, of home—and amplifies Steve’s internal turmoil. The desolate, sun-scorched landscape reflects his own fractured memories and emotional paralysis, making the return not just a physical journey but a descent into grief, betrayal, and the painful realization that the world he knew has been erased by time, war, and poison.
