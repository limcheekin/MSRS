Patti Gray, the nurse, experiences a series of physical and emotional deteriorations throughout the story as the crew faces the perilous journey to Earth. Initially, she suffers from a severe case of space-sickness and an inflamed appendix, which makes her condition critical. As the mission progresses, she endures extreme dehydration and a growing sense of dread due to the ship's failing systems—particularly the hot, fuel-tainted water, which causes her to taste and react violently to it. Her physical discomfort escalates when she discovers that the water is contaminated, leading to a moment of panic and distress. This is compounded by a sudden onset of pain and a bruise on her head, suggesting possible hallucinations or physical trauma from the ship’s unstable environment. Her psychological state deteriorates as she becomes increasingly aware of the danger surrounding the pilot, Rat, whose past record involves a crime on Mars and a controversial escape. Patti's growing anxiety is mirrored in her physical symptoms, culminating in a moment where she sees Rat hanging upside down in the chair, reinforcing the sense of disorientation and fear. Ultimately, Patti Gray’s journey reflects both the physical toll of space travel and the psychological strain of being on a desperate escape mission with a morally ambiguous pilot, leaving her vulnerable, isolated, and increasingly at risk.
