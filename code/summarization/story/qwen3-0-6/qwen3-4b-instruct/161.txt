In the story *The Man Who Rationed Babies* by Robert Silverberg, the "new world order" and the "Equalization Laws" emerge as a response to Earth's critical population crisis. By the 23rd century, Earth's population has reached seven billion, leading to severe overpopulation, resource depletion, and environmental collapse. To address this, humanity establishes a global system of population control known as the Bureau of Population Equalization (Popeek). The Equalization Laws mandate the removal of individuals deemed "substandard" — including children with genetic conditions or developmental disabilities — through a program called Happysleep, which involves euthanasia. Adults with debilitating illnesses or degenerative conditions are also sterilized or allowed to die prematurely. These policies are justified as necessary to preserve the survival and stability of the human species by eliminating genetic risks and overpopulation. The system operates under a utilitarian philosophy, where individual lives are sacrificed for the greater good of humanity. However, the story critiques this order through the moral dilemma faced by Roy Walton, the assistant administrator, who ultimately contemplates sparing one child. This moment reveals the deep ethical conflict at the heart of the new world order: while it claims to preserve life, it does so by systematically devaluing human life, especially vulnerable ones, raising profound questions about justice, individual rights, and the true cost of survival.
