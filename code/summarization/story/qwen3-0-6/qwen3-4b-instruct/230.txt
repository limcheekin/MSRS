The story centers on a journey to Mars, where the crew of various ships is driven by an obsessive ambition to conquer the planet, culminating in a deadly challenge to climb an extraordinary mountain. The mountain, however, is not a natural feature but a psychological construct created by intelligent Martians who use deep suggestion and hypnosis to manipulate human minds. The climbers believe they are ascending a towering peak that stretches endlessly into space, but in reality, the mountain is a dream-like illusion designed to test human ambition and the destructive nature of conquest. The Martians, masters of mental influence, realize that without an impossible obstacle, humanity’s drive for domination would never be curbed. By creating this mountain, they force the conquerors to confront their own inner drives, leading to madness, self-destruction, and eventual death. The narrative explores the theme of reality versus dreams, as the climbers' experiences—filled with hallucinations, visions, and delusions—blur the line between what is real and what is imagined. Bruce, a skeptical observer, begins to question the authenticity of the mountain and the crew’s fate, eventually realizing that the mountain is a psychological trap. The story ultimately reveals that human ambition, when unchecked, leads to self-annihilation, and the true reality of Mars is not a conquerable land, but a reflection of the human psyche—where dreams of conquest are as real as the illusions they create.
