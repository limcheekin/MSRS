Roddie utilizes his screwdriver and hammer as essential tools for survival, navigation, and resistance in the dystopian setting. The hammer serves as both a defensive weapon and a symbol of confrontation, used when facing threats from Invaders or hostile forces—such as when he draws it to protect himself during an attack by Ida or when attempting to stop her from carrying vital knowledge back to her people. His hammer also becomes a tool of moral reckoning, as its use is challenged by empathy and the realization that he and Ida share humanity. The screwdriver, though broken and seemingly insignificant, plays a crucial role in his problem-solving: Roddie experiments with it to test whether heat can fuse wire ends, demonstrating his ingenuity in makeshift repairs and survival tactics. This reflects his growing role as a self-reliant survivor who adapts technology to meet immediate needs. Ultimately, both tools symbolize Roddie’s human agency—his ability to act, create, and resist oppression in a world dominated by machines and dehumanizing systems. Through them, he asserts not just physical survival but a deeper sense of identity and moral responsibility.
