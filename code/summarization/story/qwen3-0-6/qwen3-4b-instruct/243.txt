Dotty’s dreams are significant because they reveal her profound, almost divine awareness of her unique existence—both as a child and as a being beyond ordinary human understanding. Her dreams reflect a deep connection to cosmic forces and a hidden truth: that she and her "god-friends" are beings who have escaped the surveillance of a larger, oppressive collective consciousness. The dreams show that Dotty perceives herself not just as a child, but as an agent of cosmic change, capable of thought and emotion that transcend time and space. Her visions of the other gods’ fear, their internal rebellion against a rigid, communal mind, and their eventual shift from hostility to openness underscore a deeper theme: the possibility of harmony between isolated, individual minds and a larger, unified reality. Dotty’s dreams also serve as a bridge between the personal and the universal—her childhood innocence becomes a vessel for cosmic wisdom. In this way, her dreams signify not only her individual awakening but also a message of hope: that even in the face of overwhelming cosmic forces, connection, empathy, and freedom of thought can prevail. Through Dotty’s dreams, the story suggests that true enlightenment lies not in domination or control, but in mutual understanding and coexistence.
