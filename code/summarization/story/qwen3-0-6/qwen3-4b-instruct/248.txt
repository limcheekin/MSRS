The setting of the story is a desolate, shifting world composed of a vast, mysterious city that exists along the floor of a canal on Mars. This city is not static—it constantly changes, flickering between beautiful, music-filled towers and grotesque, hateful forms filled with red hate and twisted architecture. The environment is dominated by red dust, rocky terrain, and an oppressive atmosphere where illusions dominate reality. The protagonist, Eric, moves through this surreal landscape, caught between physical danger and psychological torment. The city’s shifting nature reflects the protagonist’s internal confusion and fear, as the boundaries between real and imagined blur. The setting is both alien and deeply personal, mirroring the man’s fractured sense of self and memory. It exists in a liminal space—neither fully real nor entirely imagined—where new regulations (symbolizing societal or personal changes) have disrupted the familiar, and personal memories are distorted by fear and paranoia. The barren Martian landscape amplifies the sense of isolation and disorientation, reinforcing the story’s central theme: a man grappling with confusion and fear in a world where reality has been altered by both external forces and internal collapse.
