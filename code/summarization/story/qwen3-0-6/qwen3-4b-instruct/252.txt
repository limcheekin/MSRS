Herbert Quidley is a sophisticated, observant, and romantically inclined man who becomes entangled in a web of intrigue involving secret communications and an enigmatic amateur secret society known as the "snoll-doper." He is characterized by his sharp intellect, literary passion, and fascination with young women, particularly those with striking appearances—such as Kay Smith—who embody a Grecian elegance. Quidley is deeply intrigued by cryptic messages hidden in old books, especially in Taine’s *History of English Literature*, where he deciphers coded exchanges between members of the snoll-doper society. His personality blends charm and obsession; he uses subtle tactics—like the "Operation Spill-the-sugar" scheme—to initiate contact with Kay, demonstrating both his romantic ambition and his meticulous attention to detail. Though initially misinterpreting the society as a romantic or even foreign organization, he ultimately learns that the snoll-doper is a clandestine group of women (and one man) who communicate through coded messages and travel to interstellar destinations. Quidley, in turn, becomes a participant in this secret world, joining Kay and others on a journey beyond Earth. His character is thus defined by intellectual curiosity, romantic idealism, and a transformation from observer to active member of a mysterious, intimate network.
