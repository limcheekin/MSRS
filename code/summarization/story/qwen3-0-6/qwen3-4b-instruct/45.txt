The story revolves around a conformist named Martin, who lives in a tightly controlled society where individuality is suppressed and societal norms are strictly enforced. As a citizen of Northern, Martin is bound by rigid regulations, including mandatory conformity in appearance, behavior, and personal identity. His life is governed by routine, state-approved routines, and a system that prioritizes stability over personal freedom. The plot centers on Martin’s internal struggle as he begins to question his identity after experiencing dreams featuring a seductive voice urging him to defy the system—specifically, to change his name and pursue a forbidden act of self-determination. This dream becomes a catalyst for awakening, as Martin realizes that his life has been lived in suppression, and that the desire to reproduce or mate—once unthinkable—is now a deeply personal and emotional drive. His journey reflects a broader existential conflict: the tension between societal conformity and the human need for autonomy, love, and self-definition. The story critiques totalitarian control by showing how such systems erode individuality and emotion, ultimately making the act of breaking free—however small—both dangerous and essential.
