The setting of *Master of Life and Death* is a distant, dystopian future—specifically, the 23rd century—on Earth, where overpopulation has reached crisis levels, with the global population at seven billion. To address this, a rigid bureaucratic system known as the Bureau of Population Equalization (Popeek) enforces strict population control measures. The story is centered in a grim, neo-Victorian skyscraper, the Cullen Building, where Roy Walton, the assistant administrator, works in a cold, oppressive environment filled with bureaucratic red tape and moral ambiguity. This setting reflects both the technological advancement of the future and the dehumanizing effects of institutionalized control. The world is one of enforced order, where human life is treated as a variable in a larger population equation. Citizens are subjected to measures such as forced relocation, sterilization, and the euthanasia of children deemed "substandard," reinforcing a society that prioritizes efficiency and survival over individual dignity. The atmosphere is tense, oppressive, and morally complex, underscored by the emotional toll on those who enforce these policies—most notably Walton, who struggles with guilt and personal conscience as he weighs his role in a system that devalues human life. The setting thus embodies a future where technological progress and societal survival have sacrificed humanity’s most fundamental values.
