Captain Midas and Mister Spinelli share a complex, tense relationship defined by mutual suspicion and a shared obsession with the treasure of the derelict ship. Both men are driven by greed, but their motivations differ in depth and expression. Captain Midas, an aging and weary spaceman, is haunted by his past and seeks the gold not for personal gain, but as a desperate, almost symbolic redemption for a life marked by loss and failure. He initially trusts Spinelli, yet his deep-seated fear of betrayal—rooted in the dangerous nature of space and the greed that defines it—leads him to closely monitor the Third Officer.

Mister Spinelli, in contrast, is openly and aggressively greedy. He immediately recognizes the gold’s value and actively seeks to claim it for himself. When Captain Midas reveals the secret of the treasure, Spinelli disobeys orders and informs Zaleski, asserting, “I found her, and she’s mine!” This act of betrayal underscores his ambition and lack of loyalty. Their relationship shifts from one of cautious cooperation to outright conflict, culminating in a confrontation where Spinelli’s rage and avarice are exposed. Ultimately, the dynamic between them reveals a tragic truth: in a world driven by greed and danger, trust is a fragile illusion, and both men are consumed by the same insatiable desire for power and wealth.
