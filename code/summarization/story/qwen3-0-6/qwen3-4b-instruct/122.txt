The significance of time in the story lies in its role as both a narrative and philosophical framework, illustrating how attempts to alter the past through time travel ultimately reveal the fragility and inevitability of human existence. The characters grapple with the idea that their decisions—whether to eliminate ancestors or to protect them—have profound consequences across time. In one instance, Martin and Conrad use the time transmitter to go into the future and ensure a better outcome, suggesting that even small interventions can serve a purpose in correcting a flawed timeline. However, other characters, like Purnie, attempt to stop time to save lives, only to discover that if consciousness fades during the pause, time resumes without them, leading to a sense of futility and existential dread. The story underscores that time is not a malleable resource but a continuous, self-correcting stream. Any effort to alter the past risks disrupting the natural flow of events, reinforcing the idea that some outcomes are destined. Ultimately, the narrative suggests that true significance lies not in changing history, but in recognizing the value of existing moments and accepting the inevitability of time’s progression.
