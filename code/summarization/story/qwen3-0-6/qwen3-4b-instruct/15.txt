The plot of *The Plague* centers on a mysterious epidemic that spreads across the United States, affecting primarily office workers, writers, poets, artists, and newly engaged or pregnant individuals—while sparing doctors, dentists, postal workers, and rural populations. Sergeant Major Andrew McCloud, a noncom stationed in the Pentagon, is thrust into leadership when the military high command recognizes the chaos and panic caused by the outbreak. Despite initial disbelief and resistance from the brass, McCloud takes charge, investigating patterns in the illness's spread. He discovers that the victims are overwhelmingly those who frequently handle or lick stamps—particularly in small offices where stamp-licking is common among writers and secretaries. The key clue is a four-cent stamp, which links the illness to a habit of licking stamps, suggesting a chemical contaminant in the adhesive. McCloud deduces that the cause is likely a toxic substance in the glue, possibly introduced through sabotage or manufacturing error. As the story unfolds, the threat is revealed to be not a foreign invasion but a domestic anomaly tied to everyday behavior. The narrative climaxes with McCloud proposing a public warning to avoid licking stamps and urging investigations into stamp production facilities. The story ultimately resolves with the identification of the cause and a plan to contain it, emphasizing the power of observation, logic, and civilian ingenuity in solving a national crisis.
