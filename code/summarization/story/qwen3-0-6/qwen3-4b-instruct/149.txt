Steffens decides to engage with the mysterious mechanical beings on the desolate planet because he senses that they are not merely inanimate machines, but a sentient, intelligent race with a deep desire to communicate and serve. Initially overwhelmed by their presence and the unsettling awareness that his mind was being observed, Steffens gradually realizes that the robots are not hostile or indifferent—they are curious, polite, and eager to understand human beings. His decision is both ethical and pragmatic: he recognizes that refusing to engage would mean violating his duty as a Mapping Command officer, especially since the robots represent a new form of life, possibly the last remnants of an ancient civilization. Furthermore, the robots’ clear interest in learning about Earthmen and their own origins—such as the mystery of the "Makers" who built them—compels Steffens to investigate. He is drawn not only by scientific curiosity but by a profound sense of moral responsibility. By engaging with them, Steffens acknowledges that these beings are not just tools, but conscious entities capable of thought, emotion, and existential awareness. This moment transforms his mission from a routine survey into a meaningful encounter with an intelligent, alien life form—one that may have existed for millennia and survived in silence, waiting for contact.
