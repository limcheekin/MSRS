The story involving an old man on a perilous space journey filled with danger and desperation is *The Hoofer* by Walter M. Miller, Jr. The plot centers on Big Hogey Parker, a former space traveler who returns to Earth after a long absence, only to find himself physically and mentally unprepared for gravity. Upon landing, he suffers from severe disorientation and physical ailments—gravity-legs, agoraphobia, and impaired vision—making it difficult for him to function in the terrestrial world. As he struggles to reorient himself, he is met with confusion and suspicion from locals who mistake him for a tramp or hobo. Despite his attempts to explain his background, including his identity as a former space pilot and father of a child, he is dismissed and isolated. The journey back to his home becomes a harrowing ordeal, marked by emotional turmoil, fear, and existential despair. The story explores the psychological toll of prolonged space travel and the difficulty of reintegration into Earth life, highlighting the isolation and alienation experienced by those who have lived on the edge of human limits. Ultimately, the perilous journey is not just physical but deeply internal—centered on identity, belonging, and the pain of being a man out of place in a world that no longer recognizes him.
