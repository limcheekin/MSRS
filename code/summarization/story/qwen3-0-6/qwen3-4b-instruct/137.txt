Michael and Mary were sent on a mission to find a habitable planet for humanity after Earth became nearly uninhabitable due to ecological collapse and human neglect. After traveling through space for two thousand years, they discovered that Earth—once the only viable home—had been degraded beyond recovery. The mission's true objective was not just survival, but to confront the truth: humanity had destroyed its own world, and no other planet offered a sustainable future. The revelation came when they witnessed a false, idealized image of Earth on a four-dimensional screen, projecting a lie that had been maintained for generations to preserve public hope.

Their mission evolved into a moral act of defiance. Instead of accepting the false promise of salvation, they chose to return to Earth and live there, rejecting the sterile, controlled existence within the city’s force fields. They rejected the idea of another expedition, believing that human survival depended not on escaping Earth, but on rekindling connection to it—through love, life, and natural rebirth. When confronted by the council, which feared the truth of violent death and ecological ruin, Michael and Mary used their personal choice—facing death naturally on Earth—to expose the lie. Their mission, therefore, was not just one of discovery, but of resistance: to prove that humanity’s future lies not in distant planets, but in reclaiming the life, beauty, and natural balance of Earth itself. By choosing to live and die naturally on a dying planet, they became symbols of hope and renewal.
