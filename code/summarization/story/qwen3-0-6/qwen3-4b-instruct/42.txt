Mr. Crandon is an important character in the story because he represents the central figure behind the mysterious organization POSAT, which seeks to uncover and apply profound knowledge to ensure humanity's survival. Through his narrative, Crandon reveals that POSAT is not a criminal or corrupt entity but an ancient, morally grounded society founded centuries ago by a super-genius who anticipated the dangers of atomic energy and the need for human progress guided by scientific foresight. Crandon’s role as both a guide and a visionary underscores the organization’s mission: to maintain secrecy about its advanced knowledge until it can be safely used, and to selectively recruit individuals like Don who possess the right psychological makeup to understand and contribute to human motivation. His persuasive, almost paternal tone helps Don transition from disbelief to acceptance, illustrating how the organization manipulates curiosity and personal interest to attract members. Ultimately, Crandon embodies the idea that profound knowledge must be protected and cultivated over time, and that change in human behavior—through science and understanding—is possible. Without Crandon’s revelation and leadership, the story’s central themes of secrecy, moral responsibility, and the potential for human progress would lack both depth and coherence.
