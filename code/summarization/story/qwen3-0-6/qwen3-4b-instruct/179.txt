Sim gains his knowledge and absorbs his surroundings through direct, sensory immersion and the rapid processing of fleeting experiences in a world where life is brutally short. From the moment of birth, he is exposed to the harsh realities of survival—his parents’ rapid aging, the dangers of avalanches, the daily struggle for food, and the constant threat of death. These experiences are not just observed; they are internalized through vivid images and emotional reactions. Sim learns by connecting sensations with meaning: love is understood through the warmth of a mother’s touch, anger through the violence of an avalanche, and friendship through a fleeting glance with a girl named Lyte. His mind operates like a living machine, constantly interpreting and storing data in the limited time he has. He absorbs not only physical truths—such as the cycle of plant life blooming and dying—but also social and psychological ones, like how enemies are formed over stolen food or how belief systems (like the myth of gaining life energy by killing) create danger. Crucially, Sim taps into a deep, ancestral memory within himself—a racial memory—that reveals the origin of his people’s short lives: a crash from a distant green world, subjected to extreme radiation and heat. This memory allows him to understand not just his present suffering, but the possibility of escape through a preserved metal ship on a distant mountain. Thus, Sim’s knowledge is not learned slowly over time, but is forged in real-time, through trauma, emotion, and the relentless pressure of an existence measured in days. He becomes both observer and participant, absorbing every moment as if it were a vital piece of a collapsing puzzle—one that may yet offer a path to survival.
