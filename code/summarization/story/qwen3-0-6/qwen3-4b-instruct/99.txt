Michael and Mr. Carpenter represent a stark contrast in worldview and societal values. Michael, having come from a secluded, traditional human community (the Brotherhood), is naive and emotionally unprepared for the rigid, rule-bound society of Portyork. His desire to marry and form a family is immediately rejected by Carpenter, who enforces a universal taboo on marriage due to its association with slavery and exclusive possession. This reveals that Michael’s personal ideals—love, family, and individual choice—are at odds with the society’s enforced uniformity. Carpenter embodies the system of Universal Kinship, where all customs are standardized to prevent conflict, even if they seem absurd or alien to individuals like Michael. Michael’s discomfort with these rules—his confusion over eating, hand-wearing, walking, and marriage—highlights his struggle to adapt. His decision to leave Portyork is not just a rejection of society, but a retreat into his familiar values, showing that he cannot reconcile his human emotional needs with the sterile, rule-based existence promoted by Carpenter’s civilization. Thus, their relationship reflects a deeper conflict between individual freedom and universal conformity.
