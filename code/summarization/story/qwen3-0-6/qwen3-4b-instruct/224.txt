The setting of the story revolves around a harsh, isolated landscape marked by desolation and environmental upheaval, where the return of a wayfarer is met with profound personal and societal dislocation. In one key scene, the protagonist arrives in a remote, unforgiving desert terrain—characterized by bare sun-blasted rock, rippled dunes, and a "hate-haze"—where he witnesses a caravan of refugees struggling through searing conditions, pursued by hostile forces like the Kumaji. The environment is both physically and emotionally oppressive, symbolizing the alienation and danger of the journey home. This setting amplifies the emotional weight of the return: the protagonist's confusion, grief, and sense of displacement mirror the barrenness of the land. The landscape serves as a metaphor for the collapse of familiar structures—family, community, and time—highlighting how the harsh realities of survival have reshaped the world he once knew. Amidst this desolation, the journey home becomes not just a physical path, but a confrontation with loss, memory, and the fragility of human connection.
