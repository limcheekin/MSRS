Venus in the story is a harsh, alien environment dominated by extreme heat, dense clouds, and a hostile climate that has profoundly altered its native inhabitants. The Venusians are described as having undergone significant physical and societal changes due to acclimation to the planet's conditions—many appear deformed or elongated, with flabby, non-human features, suggesting adaptation to the intense heat and atmospheric pressure. They live in isolated, mist-shrouded towns, maintaining strict social codes and deep-seated suspicion of Earth visitors. Despite their outward hostility, they harbor a complex, almost mystical culture, including unique forms of energy-based life (like the Varsoom), which react to emotions such as laughter, indicating a highly sensitive, non-material consciousness. Their society is marked by tension between traditionalists and revolutionary factions, with a growing underground movement led by descendants of the first Earthmen who seek to overthrow native rule. The Venusians are portrayed as both physically alien and emotionally profound—capable of intense feelings like love and fear, which manifest in their interactions with Earth visitors. Their society is shaped by secrecy, fear, and a deep-seated desire for autonomy, making them a formidable and enigmatic presence in the story’s exploration of colonialism, identity, and revolution on a hostile world.
