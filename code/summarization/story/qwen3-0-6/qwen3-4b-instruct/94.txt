The newspapers are crucial to the story because they serve as tangible evidence that reveals the girl’s isolation from the real world and exposes the deception surrounding her identity and timeline. Mary Alice Pope claims to live in 1933, yet the newspapers Jack finds are from 1953—specifically, the yellowed, brittle edges confirm their age. This contradiction becomes pivotal when she insists on reading the papers, showing her deep immersion in a fabricated reality. The newspapers act as both a mirror and a weapon: they expose the falsity of her world, proving she has been manipulated into believing she was born in 1916 and that 1933 is the present. Jack’s ability to detect the paper’s age—its yellowing—undermines her claim and suggests a deliberate, orchestrated illusion. Thus, the newspapers are not just background detail; they are central to unraveling the mystery of her isolation, confirming that her entire life has been constructed as a lie, and demonstrating how the external world has been systematically erased from her existence.
