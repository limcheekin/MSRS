In the story, government regulations imposed on individuals are deeply tied to societal control and identity, particularly through the system of Emotional Adjustment Categories (E.A.C.) and classification based on productivity. A man struggling with memory and identity is subject to strict state-mandated rules that dictate his social role, access to resources, and even personal freedoms. For instance, individuals labeled as "Non-Productive" (N/P) are denied basic privileges such as receiving food packages or joining social groups, as their status is deemed incompatible with state efficiency. The government enforces these regulations through bureaucratic systems that require all actions to be justified by existing rules—creating a rigid, self-perpetuating structure where deviation is both monitored and punished. Attempts to change one’s name or E.A.C. are illegal, as they imply criticism of the state system. Additionally, the regulation of reproduction is strictly controlled: men cannot mate due to societal and bureaucratic disapproval of their names or status, leading to profound loneliness and psychological distress. These regulations not only limit personal autonomy but also contribute to a sense of alienation, where memory loss and identity confusion are symptoms of a society that systematically erases individuality in favor of conformity.
