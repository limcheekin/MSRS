The relationship between Herbert and his wife is defined by deep resentment, emotional distance, and mutual escapism. Herbert harbors intense hatred toward his wife, not only for the loneliness of their evenings but for her financial dominance and the sense that he was deceived in their marriage. He sees her as a symbol of control and materialism, and his hatred is fueled by jealousy and a desire for power over her wealth. However, both characters use the telporter suit as a tool for escapism—Herbert to flee into fantasy and pleasure, and his wife to step into a hidden self through the telovis and later, to materialize a version of herself in the world of escape. Her actions reveal that she too is emotionally detached, using technology to avoid reality and maintain her own private desires. The story suggests that their relationship is one of psychological alienation, where both are trapped in illusions—Herbert in his rage and fantasies, and his wife in her curated escapes. Ultimately, the narrative reveals a toxic dynamic where escapism becomes a shared, yet destructive, coping mechanism, highlighting how hidden desires and emotional neglect erode intimacy.
