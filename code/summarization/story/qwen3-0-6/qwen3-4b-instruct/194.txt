Harry Folsom is a neighbor and a figure of contrast in the story, representing a man who has found an unconventional solution to his marital struggles through escapism and the idea of an "Ego Prime"—a fictional, illegal substance that promises freedom from domestic problems. While Harry himself does not directly face marriage issues like the protagonist Sol, he serves as a symbolic influence, embodying the allure of escape and personal liberation. In the narrative, Harry is portrayed as someone whose wife is unfulfilling, prompting him to seek solace outside the home. His suggestion of an "Ego Prime" to solve all problems reflects a desire to escape emotional and relational entanglements. This idea becomes a catalyst for the protagonist’s own internal conflict, as he begins to consider similar solutions when overwhelmed by marital tension and societal constraints. Though Harry never fully succeeds in escaping his problems—his eventual journey leads to a surreal, disorienting experience that underscores the futility of such escape—he plays a crucial role in introducing the theme of seeking external fixes for personal and emotional suffering. Thus, Harry Folsom symbolizes both the temptation and the limitations of unconventional solutions in the face of deep-seated human struggles.
