The communication device in the story is central to the theme of isolation and the character's psychological unraveling. In the narrative, the communication device—specifically the ship’s interstellar link with the mother ship, *Soscites II*—serves as both a lifeline and a source of increasing paranoia. The protagonist, Kaiser, begins transmitting baby talk messages to his ship, a behavior that reflects his deteriorating mental state and growing isolation. The device's failure to process these messages properly creates a loop of confusion, highlighting the disconnect between human cognition and technological communication. As Kaiser’s symptoms worsen—fever, hallucinations, memory lapses—the device becomes a mirror of his internal decay. The baby talk, initially dismissed as a sign of illness, is later revealed to be a form of subconscious expression, indicating that the character is no longer in control of his own mind. This underscores the story’s deeper significance: isolation does not merely manifest physically but fractures the mind, and communication becomes distorted or even meaningless when the individual is severed from both external reality and internal coherence. The device thus symbolizes both connection and alienation, revealing how a lack of meaningful contact can lead to psychological collapse.
