The old man in the story holds profound symbolic significance as a figure of truth, memory, and existential revelation. In the young man's return to his childhood home, where tragedy and personal past are uncovered, the old man represents the bridge between reality and memory—between what is known and what has been hidden. His presence at the campfire, telling the story of Baba Yaga, initially seems like a simple tale, but it becomes a metaphor for the hidden dangers of the past and the power of narrative to shape perception. When the truth about their nonexistence and the fabricated reality is revealed, the old man's calm, knowing demeanor underscores his role as an observer who has seen the full scope of time and fate. He embodies the idea that some truths are not meant to be spoken until the right moment, and that silence can carry greater weight than words. His eventual admission that he "figured it all out years ago" suggests a deep understanding of time, causality, and human destiny. In this context, the old man is not just a character but a guide through the disorientation of memory and identity—showing how confronting one’s past, especially when it involves loss and erasure, can lead to a painful but necessary reckoning. His presence thus transforms the story from a simple return to a meditation on time, truth, and the human need to remember who we truly are.
