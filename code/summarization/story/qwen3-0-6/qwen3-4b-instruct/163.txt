Fred Walton is Roy Walton’s younger brother and a doctor employed by the Bureau of Population Equalization (Popeek). His significance in the narrative lies in his role as a moral and personal counterpoint to Roy’s ethical dilemma. While Roy grapples with the internal conflict of sparing one child—Philip Prior—from euthanasia, Fred represents the institutional and familial pressures that amplify the stakes of Roy’s decision. Fred is portrayed as observant and suspicious, quickly detecting Roy’s unauthorized actions when he accesses the computer records of Roy’s conversation. This reveals a deeper tension: Fred, like Roy, is part of the Popeek system, yet he embodies a more cynical or pragmatic view of its operations. His confrontation with Roy underscores the theme of betrayal within familial and institutional loyalty. Moreover, Fred’s presence highlights the story’s exploration of moral compromise—Roy risks everything to save a child, but his brother knows, and potentially intends to act against him, suggesting that even within the system, there is no true neutrality. Thus, Fred symbolizes both the internal erosion of trust and the inescapable consequences of personal choices within a rigidly controlled society.
