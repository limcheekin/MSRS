The webfooted character in the story is the "keeper of the city," a native elder who appears in the ancient ruined city. He is described as old, with long gray hair and bare feet webbed at the toes, which drag in the sand as he walks. His personality is serious, solemn, and deeply superstitious. He speaks with urgency and reverence, emphasizing that the city is sacred and must not be disturbed—any step or breath could awaken spirits and cause disaster. Despite Michaelson’s mockery and disbelief, the man remains firm and intense in his beliefs, showing a deep sense of responsibility toward the ancient site. His demeanor is both fearful and protective, reflecting a culture where spiritual forces are believed to inhabit the ruins. Though he appears rigid and stern, his actions reveal a passionate dedication to preserving the city's sanctity, suggesting that he sees himself as a guardian of something vital and eternal.
