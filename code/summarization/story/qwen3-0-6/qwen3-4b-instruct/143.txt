Individuals who lack special abilities, known as psi-deficients or "psi-negatives," are viewed with disdain and exclusion in this world where most people possess unique powers. They are seen as anomalies, burdens, and failures in a society built on telepathic and psychic capabilities. The narrative portrays the protagonist, a psi-deficient, as an outcast—labeled "an animal" and not a valuable member of society. His family avoids him not out of malice, but because his lack of power undermines their social standing and the perceived superiority of the psi-able population. Even mundane tasks are assigned to others, and he is denied employment, professional recognition, or meaningful participation in life. The community’s structure privileges those with psychic abilities, making it difficult for psi-deficients to integrate. They are excluded from advanced technologies, entertainment, and social networks, relying instead on outdated forms of recreation like reading or walking in nature. Moreover, they are often ridiculed or dismissed, such as when neighbors say, “Pity, such a talented family, to have a defective.” This systemic devaluation reflects a society where ability is not just a personal trait but a marker of worth, status, and belonging—leaving psi-deficients isolated, invisible, and emotionally alienated.
