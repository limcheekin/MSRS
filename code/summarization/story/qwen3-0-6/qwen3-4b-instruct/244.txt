Celeste’s attitude toward her family begins with a mix of worry and unease, especially in the face of cosmic uncertainty. Initially, she is unsettled by Dotty’s strange dreams and her claim to be a god, which makes Celeste feel both frightened and alienated. Her fear is not just of Dotty’s supernatural claims but of the instability they reflect—her family seems distant, almost unreal, like figures seen through a telescope. This sense of detachment grows as the story unfolds, especially when she observes her family members reacting to the astronomical crisis with varying degrees of certainty and emotion. She questions whether their family bonds are real or merely an artificial construct born of social experiment. However, by the end, Celeste’s attitude shifts from fear and skepticism to a quiet acceptance and even hope. When Dotty speaks of a peaceful reunion with her “pursuers” and a new societal harmony, Celeste’s face softens, and she begins to see the possibility of unity and meaning beyond science or logic. This transformation reflects her growing belief that, despite cosmic chaos, love and connection—especially familial—can endure and even transcend fear. Her final reflection on the Earth's movement like a ship suggests a deeper emotional truth: in facing the unknown, she finds peace not in certainty, but in the enduring strength of her family bonds.
