The story takes place in multiple interconnected settings that reflect the characters' personal struggles and encounters with time travel. The primary setting is a mysterious, enigmatic "station" run by Mr. Snader—a middle-class home in a peaceful neighborhood with warm dusk lighting and distant mountain views. Inside, the environment is eerie yet inviting: a waiting room with two large movie screens displaying endless corridors labeled "Ante" and "Post," symbolizing the flow of time. This setting functions as a surreal representation of time travel, where the characters move through numbered stations (701 to 725) without physical sensation, suggesting a fictional or metaphysical experience rather than literal time travel. 

A secondary setting emerges when the protagonist, Simon, returns to a familiar environment—his office in a city—but finds Mr. Oyster, a man who has been waiting for him for days, and discovers that the calendar has been altered. This scene underscores the paradox of time travel: if someone returns from the future, their presence alters the timeline, causing confusion and disorientation. These contrasting settings—both the technological, dreamlike station and the mundane, grounded office—highlight the story’s central theme: the personal and psychological toll of confronting time, and the impossibility of changing the past without disrupting reality. The narrative uses these locations to explore themes of regret, identity, and the fear of altering one’s fate.
