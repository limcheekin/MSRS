The society depicted in this story is structured around a post-apocalyptic future where civilization has been fundamentally transformed by trauma and technological advancement. After Earth’s destruction due to unchecked violence and overpopulation, survivors migrated to new worlds, such as Tintera, where they live under a highly controlled, pacified regime. In this society, children are raised in an environment devoid of conflict, where war and violence are no longer part of daily life. Instead, all problems are resolved through automation and state-mandated emotional conditioning—children are trained to reject violent solutions from an early age, ensuring a peaceful, stable society. However, this utopia is deeply questioned by characters like Martin and the Butcher, who observe that such peace may be artificial, built on the suppression of natural human instincts. The society’s structure reflects a paradox: while it claims to have achieved harmony through technology and pacification, it also relies on fear, surveillance, and enforced compliance. The presence of advanced technology—such as scoutships, automated systems, and the Time Theater—suggests a civilization that has moved beyond physical conflict but remains psychologically constrained. Thus, the society is not truly free from violence; rather, it has internalized and repressed it, raising ethical questions about the cost of peace and the role of trauma in shaping human progress.
