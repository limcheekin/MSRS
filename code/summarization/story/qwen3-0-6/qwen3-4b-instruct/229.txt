The setting of the story involving a man's unsettling discovery in a town where something sinister seems to be happening around him is a remote, isolated rural community with a strange and decaying atmosphere. This is most clearly illustrated in *The Haunted Fountain*, where the protagonist, Maitland, arrives at a mysterious, seemingly ordinary house with an unnaturally designed interior—walls of blue plastic, smooth metal doors without knobs, and a transparent, unbreakable window that reveals a peaceful river scene. The unsettling elements are amplified by the strange sight of two muscular, tan-skinned individuals swimming toward the house, which contradicts the rural, unremarkable setting. The town's silence, its lack of familiar landmarks, and the subtle distortions in time and reality—such as the fading of footprints and the man’s growing disorientation—create a sense of something unnatural lurking beneath the surface. The presence of a town with no visible signs of modern life or development, combined with the inexplicable occurrences, suggests a hidden, possibly supernatural force at work, making the rural setting feel both familiar and deeply foreboding. This atmosphere of creeping dread and dislocation defines the story’s unsettling tone.
