Barry Barr undergoes profound physical and psychological transformations throughout his journey on the hostile planet Venus. Initially, exposure to Sigma radiation causes severe bodily changes, including the spread of membranous webs between his fingers and toes, darkened skin, and pronounced neck swellings. These changes signal a deterioration of his human form. When he survives a near-fatal accident in the ship’s jet room, he is trapped in a sealed room with power and water supplies cut off, forcing him to endure extreme isolation and panic. This leads to a critical shift: after being submerged in Venusian swamps, he develops full underwater breathing capabilities, resulting in the emergence of rudimentary gills. His body becomes partially alien, no longer fully reliant on Earth-based respiration. This transformation makes him physically distinct from his fellow colonists, severing his connection to human norms and creating a gulf both biological and existential. Psychologically, Barry experiences intense instability—frequent bouts of depression, hallucinations, and moments of delusion—especially during his isolation. His bond with Dorothy strengthens, offering emotional grounding and a sense of identity. However, his growing alienation from Earthly life and the presence of hostile Venusian life forms, including venomous worms and amphibious creatures, further cement his transformation. Ultimately, Barry evolves from a human into a being adapted to the planet’s environment, symbolizing both survival and the loss of his original self.
