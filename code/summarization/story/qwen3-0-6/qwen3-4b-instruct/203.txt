The theme of global warming is explored in the narrative through a scientist's invention—the Expendable field device—that unintentionally contributes to rising planetary temperatures. The AEC agent reveals that this device appears to violate the law of conservation of energy by destroying matter without producing energy, yet it actually converts the energy released during matter destruction into heat. This heat accumulates in Earth’s planetary potential, causing a dangerous rise in global temperature. The narrative presents this as a direct, scientific threat: if unchecked, the continued use of such devices could heat Earth to a point of combustion. The scientist, however, counters the urgency by suggesting that the excess energy can be neutralized through specially designed engines that consume the heat. This illustrates the theme not as a distant, abstract phenomenon, but as a tangible, immediate consequence of human technological choices. The story underscores how even well-intentioned inventions can have unintended environmental consequences, and how scientific understanding must guide policy to prevent ecological catastrophe. Thus, global warming is portrayed as both a real, measurable threat and a result of human innovation gone unregulated.
