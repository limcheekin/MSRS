The Earthmen are desolate in Zur because their hopes for revival—once filled with promise and technological advancement—are systematically undermined by the sinister, exploitative nature of Earth's influence. While the Earthmen initially arrive with grand intentions of trade and progress, they soon establish corporations that dominate Zurian markets, replacing local crafts like ceramic ware with inferior, mass-produced goods. Their control extends beyond commerce: they monopolize information through advertisements and newspapers, manipulate public perception, and exploit natural resources like oil and gas without transparency or consent. The introduction of electric appliances, television, and modern infrastructure leads to financial dependency and cultural erosion, as traditional ways of life are replaced by Earth-centric consumerism. Moreover, the Earthmen ultimately withdraw their support, leaving the Zurians without contracts or economic stability. This betrayal, combined with the loss of autonomy and the suppression of local industries, leaves the people—especially those like the Masur brothers—feeling alienated, impoverished, and spiritually hollow. Thus, the desolation stems not from lack of opportunity, but from a deliberate, insidious exploitation that erodes both economic self-sufficiency and cultural identity.
