The story takes place on a remote, isolated island off the coast of Maine. The setting is a narrow, rocky cove where the protagonist ventures to explore the farthest island out, describing the environment as quiet and strangely still, as if time itself has paused. The island is surrounded by the Atlantic Ocean, and the narrative emphasizes the isolation and eerie stillness of the location. This coastal, rocky island setting is central to the story's atmosphere, reinforcing themes of solitude, mystery, and the uncanny. The protagonist's journey begins with a personal desire to reach the edge of known land, and the island's untouched, timeless quality deepens the sense of wonder and unease. The physical description of the cove, with its "glassy" surface and fading footprints, further underscores the idea that time and reality have been disrupted, making the location both a literal and symbolic place of transition. Thus, the story unfolds in a secluded, coastal island near Maine, where natural isolation and an inexplicable stillness create a haunting backdrop for the protagonist's experience.
