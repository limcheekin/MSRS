The plot revolves around a group of explorers who land on the seemingly idyllic planet Minos, only to discover it is already inhabited by a small, isolated colony of human-like beings. The explorers are initially shocked to learn that the planet has been settled for three generations with no contact from Earth or any external traders. A key figure, Patrick Mead, reveals that the colony was established after a devastating plague known as the "melting sickness," which killed nearly all inhabitants except the Mead family, who were immune. This immunity led to a genetic uniformity—everyone on Minos looks like the Meads—because the survivors were closely related and shared similar traits. The explorers, concerned about contagion due to the history of plague planets, conduct cautious medical tests to ensure safety. Despite the planet's peaceful appearance and breathable air, the presence of the disease and its origin raise critical questions about survival, biological evolution, and the risks of contact between isolated populations. The story ultimately explores themes of survival, the dangers of assuming safety in unfamiliar environments, and the possibility that a seemingly perfect world may conceal a hidden, inherited threat.
