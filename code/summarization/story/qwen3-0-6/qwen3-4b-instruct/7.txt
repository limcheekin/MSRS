Larry Dobbin is a fellow space explorer and the best friend of Willard, the sole survivor of the spacecraft *Mary Lou*. In the story, Dobbin is ill and dying in space, suffering from the physical and emotional toll of prolonged isolation. His final moments are marked by a profound longing to return to Earth—specifically, to walk on solid ground again—highlighting the central theme of human connection and the psychological agony of solitude in space. As he nears death, Dobbin experiences a hallucination of the "Ghost Ship," a legendary vessel said to appear to space travelers who have died alone in the void. He insists he sees it, believing it is a sign that it has come for him. Though Willard initially dismisses the vision as a delusion, the story suggests it may be a real phenomenon tied to the loneliness of space. Ultimately, Dobbin dies, and Willard is left alone in the ship, haunted by memories of Earth and the ghostly presence of his friend. The Ghost Ship remains a haunting symbol of the unresolved grief and existential dread faced by those who live beyond Earth's reach.
