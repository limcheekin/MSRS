The setting of the story is an ancient, mysterious city built by a long-lost civilization on a lifeless planet, half a million years ago. It is described as alien yet strangely familiar, with geometric precision, metallic structures, and a haunting stillness. The city lies in a barren, desolate landscape, surrounded by vast sand dunes and an eerie twilight sky. Its architecture—ranging from tall spires to square and spheroid buildings—is both beautiful and unsettling, with ruins preserved through time despite exposure to wind and weather. The environment is silent and oppressive, emphasizing the city’s isolation and abandonment. Despite its decay, the setting pulses with subtle signs of life: warm objects like a clock, voices in books, and the presence of spirits or ancestral forces that seem to linger. The city feels sacred, not to gods, but to something older and more profound—perhaps consciousness, memory, or lost intelligence. It is a place of deep mystery, where time seems distorted, and where the boundary between the physical and the metaphysical blurs, making it both a tomb and a living presence.
