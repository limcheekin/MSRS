The other humans, or "hoofers," help Hogey get home by showing compassion and offering practical support despite his disoriented and unstable state. After Hogey stumbles and falls on the bus, two men help him onto the rear seat and ensure his gin bottle is safely stored. When he becomes disruptive and agitated, they gently guide him back to calmness. The bus driver, upon noticing Hogey’s distress, stops the bus and helps him safely exit at Caine’s junction. Later, when Hogey is too weak to walk, a farmer picks him up in his truck, recognizing him as Big Hogey Parker and helping him to Marie’s home. Even after Hogey collapses near the house, the farmer and a woman passenger—though initially indifferent—eventually assist by offering care and guidance. The most crucial act of support comes when the farmer, upon learning Hogey is married to Marie, refuses to let him walk alone and drives him directly to her family’s farmhouse. These interactions demonstrate that the "hoofers" are not only tolerant of Hogey’s strange behavior but actively intervene to ensure his safe return to family, reflecting a community grounded in empathy and mutual care.
