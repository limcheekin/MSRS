Captain Terrence gradually descends into psychological and mental breakdown throughout the story. Initially driven by an obsessive desire for conquest, he becomes increasingly delusional and unhinged as the expedition climbs higher and higher on Mars. His reports grow more erratic, filled with grandiose claims of transcending human limits and reaching "the top of the universe." He justifies violent actions—such as shooting his own crew member Anhauser for dissenting—under the guise of maintaining order and testing human resilience. As the climb continues without a visible end, Terrence’s reality begins to fracture. His voice becomes strained, then incoherent, eventually breaking into "crazy yells" that fade away. By the time Bruce finally hears from him again, Terrence’s communication is no longer reliable or coherent. Ultimately, Terrence is found dead, clinging to the mountain with his arms stretched out, a symbol of both physical and psychological collapse. His fate reflects the story's central theme: the danger of unchecked ambition and the psychological toll of isolation and infinite ascent. The mountain, which appears to have no summit, becomes a metaphor for the unattainable and destructive pursuit of power and conquest.
