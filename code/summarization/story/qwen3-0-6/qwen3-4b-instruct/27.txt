In this society, technology plays a central but passive role—serving humanity rather than driving it. Unlike the past, where machines were seen as tools for conquest or exploration, in this world, technology is fully integrated and automated, eliminating the need for physical labor. People live in comfort and leisure, with no wars, poverty, or hardship, as machinery handles all work. This technological advancement has led to a profound shift in human values: instead of focusing on exploration, space travel, or scientific discovery, society now prioritizes human experience, culture, and emotional connection. The people of this world are deeply interested in the study of human life, psychology, and cultural history—what Swarts calls the "Age of Man." As a result, the fascination with space and adventure has faded. Technology is no longer a source of wonder or aspiration; it is taken for granted. This shift reflects a societal choice to reject the risks and challenges of the past in favor of stability, peace, and personal fulfillment. Thus, technology in this world is not a driver of progress but a means of enabling a serene, self-sufficient existence where human relationships and cultural understanding take precedence over expansion or innovation.
