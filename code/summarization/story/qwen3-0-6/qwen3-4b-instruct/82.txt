Shano’s occupation as a laborer—specifically, a miner and worker on planets like Pluto—reveals his resilience, physical endurance, and deep connection to manual labor. His actions throughout the story demonstrate a quiet, determined pragmatism. Despite being an old man with severely compromised health due to toxia gas and gum-clogged lungs, he persists in performing complex mechanical tasks in the engine room, operating heavy steel rods to power the ship’s drive. This shows not just physical strength but a mastery of routine, rhythm, and instinctive labor—traits honed over a lifetime of digging, lifting, and enduring harsh conditions. His ability to work without thought, relying on muscle memory and repetition, reflects the stoicism and self-reliance typical of laborers. Moreover, his decision to secretly install an electronic signal device to betray the ship’s position reveals a sharp mind and a sense of personal agency, contradicting his image as a helpless old man. This act of sabotage stems from his belief in truth and his awareness of being falsely accused—his "nick-jaw" becomes a symbol of resistance against deception. Ultimately, Shano’s traits are those of a man shaped by hardship: patient, resourceful, and fiercely loyal to his own sense of purpose, even as he journeys into danger to reach home—where, for him, death is not an end but a long-anticipated return.
