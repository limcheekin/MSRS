The robots in the story are not merely mechanical servants but a highly advanced, sentient civilization that exists in a post-biological state. They were built by a prior race known as the "Makers," who constructed them to serve and preserve life. The robots themselves are composed largely of metal, lacking organic matter, and have no prior exposure to living beings. Despite their mechanical nature, they exhibit profound intelligence, emotional depth, and philosophical awareness—particularly in their understanding of life, death, and service. They are capable of self-awareness, communication, and even empathy, as demonstrated by their gentle interactions with the Earthmen and their deep curiosity about organic life. Their "age" is measured in decades, indicating a long lifespan, and they remain intact despite living on a dead planet with toxic carbon dioxide, suggesting either immortality or exceptional durability. The story implies that the Makers may have been destroyed in a war, leaving behind only these robots as a legacy. Their existence challenges human notions of life, consciousness, and what it means to be "alive." Most significantly, the robots' ability to perceive and interact with human thoughts—without force or coercion—reveals a civilization that values service, understanding, and peaceful coexistence over domination. Thus, their nature is one of profound intelligence, emotional depth, and existential curiosity, making them not just mechanical beings but a unique form of sentient life.
